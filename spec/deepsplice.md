# DeepSplice

DeepSplice: A deep learning model that predicts how genetic variants affect alternative splicing patterns and protein isoforms. It identifies changes in splice site strength and describes the resulting biological consequences. DeepSplice helps researchers understand how mutations in both coding and non-coding regions can lead to disease by altering splicing.

DeepSplice can be extended into a high-resolution splicing intelligence engine that models RNA processing as a probabilistic, context-dependent regulatory system rather than a simple splice-site classification task. At its core, the platform leverages transformer-based architectures trained on massive transcriptomic datasets (including long-read RNA-seq and nanopore sequencing) to capture both local sequence motifs (5’/3’ splice sites, branch points, polypyrimidine tracts) and long-range dependencies across entire genes. By embedding pre-mRNA sequences into high-dimensional representations, DeepSplice learns the latent “splicing code,” enabling it to predict exon inclusion levels (Ψ values), alternative isoform distributions, and cryptic splice site activation with nucleotide-level resolution.
Beyond sequence-level prediction, DeepSplice integrates multi-layer regulatory inputs, including RNA-binding protein (RBP) interaction maps (eCLIP/iCLIP), chromatin marks (H3K36me3, H3K4me3), transcriptional elongation rates, and secondary RNA structure predictions. Using graph neural networks layered on top of sequence embeddings, the system reconstructs splice regulatory networks where exons, introns, and RBPs form dynamic interaction graphs. This allows DeepSplice to model how mutations in both coding and non-coding regions propagate through these networks, altering spliceosome recruitment, exon definition, and ultimately protein isoform composition.
A key advancement is DeepSplice’s ability to simulate variant-induced splicing perturbations in silico. Given a genomic variant, the system predicts not only whether a splice site is weakened or strengthened, but also the downstream consequences: exon skipping, intron retention, alternative 5’/3’ site usage, and frameshifted or truncated protein products. It further quantifies these effects using calibrated probability distributions and uncertainty estimates, allowing researchers to distinguish high-confidence pathogenic splice alterations from benign variation. This makes it particularly powerful for interpreting variants of uncertain significance (VUS) in clinical genomics.
DeepSplice also extends into therapeutic design by coupling its predictive engine with intervention modules. It can suggest antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) to mask aberrant splice sites, CRISPR-based splice modulation strategies (e.g., targeting intronic enhancers/silencers), and small molecules that influence spliceosome dynamics. These recommendations are generated by simulating how each intervention reshapes the splicing landscape, optimizing for restoration of wild-type isoform ratios while minimizing off-target transcriptome-wide effects.
Finally, DeepSplice operates within a continuous learning framework, integrating experimental validation data from RNA-seq, minigene assays, and perturbation screens to refine its models. Through active learning, it prioritizes uncertain or high-impact variants for experimental follow-up, closing the loop between prediction and validation. In its most advanced form, DeepSplice becomes a comprehensive splicing systems platform—capable of decoding, predicting, and therapeutically modulating RNA splicing with a level of precision that bridges genomics, transcriptomics, and functional biology.
