# Robotic Flow

Robotic Flow: A control interface for lab automation hardware that coordinates high-throughput tasks like transformation and plating. It manages real-time monitoring and ensures that experimental protocols are executed with high precision. Robotic Flow integrates with robotic platforms, accelerating the transition from digital genetic designs to physical samples for manufacturing.

Robotic Flow operates as the real-time control and coordination layer for laboratory robotics, managing the execution of complex biological workflows with high temporal and spatial precision. It treats the laboratory as a distributed cyber-physical system, where robotic arms, liquid handlers, incubators, and sensors function as interconnected nodes. The platform uses scheduling algorithms and constraint solvers to allocate tasks, optimize throughput, and minimize idle time, ensuring efficient utilization of lab resources.
At the operational level, Robotic Flow implements fine-grained control over experimental steps such as pipetting, plating, transformation, and colony picking. It models fluid dynamics at the microscale—accounting for viscosity, surface tension, and evaporation—to ensure accurate liquid handling, particularly in nanoliter-scale reactions. Error detection mechanisms, including computer vision and sensor feedback, identify anomalies such as pipetting errors, contamination, or equipment malfunctions, triggering corrective actions in real time.
From a biological perspective, the system maintains strict control over environmental conditions critical for cell growth and experimental reproducibility. It dynamically regulates incubation temperatures, shaking speeds, and gas compositions (e.g., oxygen levels in fermentation) to match protocol requirements. It also tracks sample lineage and metadata, ensuring traceability across multi-step workflows and enabling precise correlation between experimental conditions and outcomes.
Chemically, Robotic Flow manages reagent handling and storage, tracking stability, expiration, and compatibility. It ensures that sensitive reagents—enzymes, nucleotides, substrates—are handled under optimal conditions, preventing degradation or cross-contamination. For assays involving chemical reactions, it controls timing and mixing precision to maintain consistent reaction kinetics across large experimental batches.
By integrating real-time control, automation hardware, and biological context, Robotic Flow enables seamless execution of high-throughput experiments. It acts as the physical execution backbone of automated labs, ensuring that complex synthetic biology workflows are carried out with precision, reproducibility, and scalability.
