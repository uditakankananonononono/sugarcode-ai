# MutDock

MutDock: A predictive module that models how point mutations in a protein alter its affinity for existing drugs. It identifies resistance hotspots and forecasts cross-drug resistance across therapeutic alternatives. MutDock helps researchers and clinicians understand the molecular basis of drug resistance, enabling the development of personalized and effective treatment plans.

MutDock can be rigorously framed as a mutation-aware perturbation model over protein–ligand interaction landscapes, where the central objective is to quantify how a sequence perturbation ΔS\Delta SΔS induces a shift in binding free energy ΔΔGbind\Delta \Delta G_{\text{bind}}ΔΔGbind​. Formally, for a wild-type structure xWT\mathbf{x}_{WT}xWT​ and mutant xmut\mathbf{x}_{mut}xmut​, the system estimates:
ΔΔG=ΔGbindmut−ΔGbindWT\Delta \Delta G = \Delta G_{\text{bind}}^{mut} - \Delta G_{\text{bind}}^{WT}ΔΔG=ΔGbindmut​−ΔGbindWT​
This is computed using a hybrid scoring function that combines physics-based energy terms (van der Waals, electrostatics, solvation) with learned potentials derived from graph neural networks trained on large-scale mutagenesis and binding datasets. MutDock treats mutations as localized perturbations in a high-dimensional energy landscape, propagating their effects through structural rearrangements and interaction networks.
At the structural level, MutDock integrates models from tools like AlphaFold to generate mutant conformations, followed by local refinement using energy minimization and side-chain repacking algorithms. The protein–ligand complex is represented as a heterogeneous graph G=(V,E)G = (V, E)G=(V,E), where nodes correspond to atoms/residues and edges encode spatial proximity, chemical bonds, and interaction potentials. Graph neural networks operate over this structure to learn how mutations alter interaction fingerprints—such as hydrogen bonding networks, hydrophobic packing, and π–π stacking—providing a fine-grained map of disrupted or newly formed contacts.
MutDock further incorporates molecular docking and conformational sampling to account for induced fit effects. Instead of assuming a static binding pose, the system performs ensemble docking across multiple mutant conformations, approximating the distribution P(xligand∣xprotein)P(\mathbf{x}_{ligand} | \mathbf{x}_{protein})P(xligand​∣xprotein​). This allows it to capture cases where mutations reshape the binding pocket geometry, altering ligand orientation or accessibility. Advanced sampling techniques such as Monte Carlo and normal mode perturbations are used to explore conformational flexibility, while learned scoring functions rank poses based on predicted affinity.
From a systems perspective, MutDock extends beyond single mutation analysis to model combinatorial mutational landscapes. Using techniques from statistical learning and epistasis modeling, it predicts non-linear interactions between multiple mutations, identifying synergistic or compensatory effects that influence drug resistance. This is particularly relevant in oncology and infectious disease, where resistance often arises from multi-site mutations. The system can construct mutational fitness landscapes, mapping regions of high resistance potential and suggesting alternative therapeutic targets or drug modifications.
Finally, MutDock integrates into a broader computational pipeline, generating outputs such as ΔΔG distributions, structural annotations of mutation-induced changes, and ranked ligand sensitivity profiles. It supports downstream applications including personalized medicine (predicting patient-specific drug response), drug design (identifying mutation-resilient compounds), and evolutionary forecasting (anticipating resistance pathways). By combining thermodynamics, structural biology, graph-based AI, and statistical modeling, MutDock becomes a powerful engine for understanding and counteracting mutation-driven changes in molecular interactions.
