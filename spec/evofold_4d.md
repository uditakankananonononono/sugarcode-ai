# EvoFold 4D

EvoFold 4D: A dynamics engine that predicts protein conformational transitions, such as moving between open and closed states. It models the impact of post-translational modifications and ligand binding on structural flexibility. EvoFold 4D provides temporal traces of morphological changes, offering a four-dimensional view of how protein function is governed.

EvoFold 4D can be formalized as a spatiotemporal protein dynamics engine that models conformational landscapes as trajectories over a high-dimensional manifold M⊂R3N×Rt\mathcal{M} \subset \mathbb{R}^{3N} \times \mathbb{R}^tM⊂R3N×Rt, where NNN is the number of atoms and ttt represents time. Instead of predicting a single static structure, the system learns a probability distribution P(x,t)P(\mathbf{x}, t)P(x,t) over conformations, effectively approximating the protein’s free energy landscape. Using neural stochastic differential equations (Neural SDEs) and diffusion-based generative models, EvoFold 4D simulates transitions between conformational states (e.g., open ↔ closed), capturing both equilibrium fluctuations and rare but functionally critical transitions such as allosteric shifts.
At the core of the system lies a hybrid modeling framework that combines deep learning with molecular dynamics (MD). Neural networks—trained on trajectory data and structural ensembles—learn coarse-grained force fields Fθ(x)F_\theta(\mathbf{x})Fθ​(x), which approximate the gradient of the potential energy surface −∇E(x)-\nabla E(\mathbf{x})−∇E(x). These learned forces are integrated over time using numerical solvers (e.g., Langevin dynamics), enabling efficient simulation of protein motion without the full computational cost of atomistic MD. EvoFold 4D augments this with physics-based constraints (bond lengths, angles, dihedral potentials), ensuring that generated trajectories remain chemically realistic.
The platform incorporates concepts from statistical mechanics to quantify conformational stability and transition kinetics. Free energy differences between states are estimated using Boltzmann-weighted distributions:

allowing the system to compute transition probabilities, identify metastable basins, and estimate kinetic rates using methods such as Markov State Models (MSMs). By discretizing conformational space into states and learning transition matrices, EvoFold 4D provides a coarse-grained yet interpretable view of protein dynamics, highlighting dominant pathways and bottlenecks in conformational change.
A key innovation is the integration of evolutionary and sequence-based information into dynamic predictions. By embedding sequences using protein language models and co-evolutionary signals (from MSAs), EvoFold 4D conditions its dynamic simulations on evolutionary constraints. This allows it to predict how mutations alter not just static structure but the entire conformational landscape—shifting energy minima, altering transition barriers, and potentially inducing loss or gain of function. The system can thus simulate mutation-driven dynamics, providing insights into mechanisms of drug resistance or altered enzymatic activity.
Finally, EvoFold 4D functions as an end-to-end computational environment, generating temporal structural trajectories (multi-frame PDBs), energy landscape visualizations, and transition networks. It integrates with docking and mutation analysis modules, enabling downstream workflows such as ligand binding in different conformational states or screening mutations for stability and flexibility. By unifying deep generative modeling, molecular physics, and evolutionary biology, EvoFold 4D becomes a four-dimensional window into protein behavior—capturing not just what a protein looks like, but how it moves, adapts, and functions over time.
