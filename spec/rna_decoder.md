# RNA Decoder

RNA Decoder: A tool for predicting epitranscriptomic modifications like m6A from sequence data, using models trained on nanopore datasets. It visualizes modification maps and simulates the impact of synthetic RNA edits on translation efficiency. RNA Decoder allows for the design of optimized mRNA sequences, enhancing stability and expression for therapeutics.

RNA Decoder can be elevated into a fully integrated epitranscriptomic inference and design engine that models RNA not merely as a linear sequence, but as a dynamically modified, structurally adaptive, and translation-coupled information layer. At its core, the system leverages multi-modal deep learning architectures trained on nanopore direct RNA sequencing, miCLIP, and MeRIP-seq datasets to detect and quantify RNA modifications such as m6A, m5C, Ψ (pseudouridine), and m1A at single-nucleotide resolution. These models go beyond signal classification by incorporating raw ionic current traces and basecalling uncertainty profiles, enabling de novo identification of modification signatures even in poorly annotated transcriptomes. The resulting outputs are probabilistic modification maps that capture both site occupancy and stoichiometry across different cellular conditions.
The platform integrates RNA secondary and tertiary structure prediction directly into its modification analysis pipeline, recognizing that many epitranscriptomic marks are structurally contextual. RNA Decoder uses thermodynamic folding algorithms augmented with graph neural networks to model base-pairing interactions and higher-order motifs, then overlays modification likelihoods to identify structurally constrained modification hotspots. It simulates how modifications alter local folding energy landscapes, ribosome accessibility, and RNA–protein interaction interfaces, enabling predictions of how specific edits influence transcript stability, splicing efficiency, and translation rates. This creates a unified framework where sequence, structure, and chemical modification are jointly optimized.
A key innovation is its ability to model co-transcriptional and post-transcriptional dynamics of RNA modifications. By integrating time-series datasets and transcriptional kinetics, RNA Decoder reconstructs modification deposition and removal processes mediated by writer (e.g., METTL3/14), eraser (e.g., FTO, ALKBH5), and reader proteins. It builds kinetic models that predict how environmental signals or cellular states shift modification landscapes over time, allowing users to simulate perturbations such as enzyme knockdowns or overexpression. This temporal dimension is critical for understanding transient regulatory events, such as stress responses or developmental transitions, where RNA modifications act as rapid control mechanisms.
RNA Decoder also functions as a design engine for synthetic RNA therapeutics, optimizing sequences for stability, translational efficiency, and immunogenicity. It can propose synonymous codon substitutions and UTR engineering strategies that modulate ribosome loading, RNA half-life, and interaction with innate immune sensors. The system simulates how specific modification patterns—such as pseudouridine incorporation in mRNA vaccines—affect translation kinetics and immune activation, enabling rational design of RNA constructs with desired expression profiles. It further evaluates delivery compatibility by predicting how sequence features influence encapsulation efficiency and degradation in different delivery systems.
At the systems level, RNA Decoder outputs comprehensive epitranscriptomic profiles, including modification maps, structural annotations, and predicted functional impacts, alongside experimentally actionable designs. These outputs can be exported into formats compatible with downstream validation methods such as nanopore sequencing pipelines, ribosome profiling, or CLIP-seq experiments. By integrating signal-level data, structural modeling, and dynamic regulation, RNA Decoder operates as a full-stack interpreter and engineer of the RNA layer, enabling researchers to decode and manipulate the epitranscriptome with a level of precision that parallels advances in genome editing.
