# CellFateNet

CellFateNet: A visualization engine for the causal regulatory networks and lineage transitions that drive cellular differentiation and reprogramming. It identifies key regulatory nodes and provides a step-by-step genetic "recipe" for transformation. CellFateNet offers deep insights into molecular mechanisms governing identity, helping researchers manipulate cell states for therapeutic purposes.

Bio-Switch
Bio-Switch evolves from a simple biosensor design tool into a fully programmable molecular signal transduction compiler that operates across thermodynamic, kinetic, and genetic layers of biological systems. At its core, it models ligand–receptor interactions using binding free energy landscapes (ΔG = −RT ln K_d) and integrates quantum-informed docking approximations with coarse-grained molecular dynamics to predict how small molecules, ions, or environmental signals induce conformational changes in sensing domains such as riboswitches, transcription factors, or engineered allosteric proteins. These conformational transitions are then translated into probabilistic gene expression outputs through stochastic modeling frameworks like the Chemical Master Equation, allowing Bio-Switch to simulate noise, burst kinetics, and threshold responses in real cellular environments.
Beyond sensing, Bio-Switch functions as a circuit-level compiler that maps molecular recognition events into executable genetic programs using Boolean logic, fuzzy logic, and even analog signal processing paradigms. It encodes biosensor outputs into transcriptional cascades, CRISPR-based transcriptional regulators (dCas9 activators/repressors), or RNA-based switches, optimizing promoter strengths and ribosome binding sites via gradient descent over expression fitness landscapes. The system leverages information theory—quantifying signal fidelity via mutual information (I(X;Y))—to ensure that biosensors maximize distinguishability between input states, which is critical for applications like detecting low-abundance metabolites or distinguishing between structurally similar toxins.
Mathematically, Bio-Switch integrates nonlinear dynamical systems modeling using ordinary differential equations (ODEs) and stochastic differential equations (SDEs) to simulate time-resolved responses. It solves coupled reaction networks representing ligand binding, transcription, translation, and degradation using numerical solvers (e.g., Runge–Kutta methods), allowing users to visualize phase space trajectories, bistability, and oscillatory dynamics. These simulations are augmented with Bayesian inference layers that update model parameters in real time based on experimental feedback, effectively turning Bio-Switch into a self-correcting system identification engine for biological circuits.
From a synthetic biology perspective, Bio-Switch integrates directly with chassis-specific constraints, incorporating host-specific transcription factor compatibility, codon usage biases, and metabolic burden models. It predicts how biosensor circuits interact with host metabolism using flux balance analysis (FBA), ensuring that sensing systems do not perturb essential pathways. Additionally, it supports multiplexed sensing architectures, designing orthogonal sensor arrays that minimize crosstalk through sequence-level and structural orthogonality constraints, enabling high-dimensional environmental monitoring within a single engineered cell.
Ultimately, Bio-Switch becomes a universal interface between chemistry and computation in living systems—transforming molecular events into programmable outputs with quantified reliability. Its ability to integrate thermodynamics, kinetics, information theory, and genetic engineering allows it to design biosensors not just as detection tools, but as decision-making units within larger biological machines, enabling applications ranging from smart therapeutics that activate only in disease microenvironments to environmental microbes that dynamically respond to pollutants with engineered metabolic responses.

PhageForge
PhageForge extends bacteriophage engineering into a computationally rigorous platform that treats phages as programmable nanoscale delivery systems governed by evolutionary dynamics, structural biology, and systems-level host interactions. At the genomic level, it models phage genomes as modular architectures, applying graph-based genome editing frameworks where nodes represent functional modules (capsid proteins, tail fibers, lytic enzymes, CRISPR payloads) and edges encode regulatory dependencies. Using constraint optimization and combinatorial search algorithms, PhageForge designs recombinant phages that maximize host specificity while maintaining replication fitness, solving a multi-objective optimization problem balancing infectivity, payload capacity, and evolutionary stability.
A key innovation lies in its integration of receptor-binding protein (RBP) engineering with structural prediction. PhageForge uses protein structure models and docking simulations to redesign tail fiber proteins, optimizing binding affinity to bacterial surface receptors through energy minimization and machine-learned scoring functions. These interactions are modeled using molecular mechanics approximations (e.g., Lennard-Jones potentials, electrostatic interactions), enabling precise tuning of host range. The system also incorporates population genetics models, simulating phage–bacteria coevolution using differential equations and agent-based models to predict resistance emergence and guide the design of phage cocktails that minimize evolutionary escape pathways.
PhageForge embeds CRISPR payloads within phage genomes, designing guide RNAs that target antibiotic resistance genes, virulence factors, or essential bacterial loci. It optimizes CRISPR cassette placement within the phage genome to ensure proper expression timing during infection cycles, integrating transcriptional timing models and RNA folding predictions. The platform also simulates intracellular dynamics post-infection, modeling how CRISPR-mediated cleavage interacts with bacterial repair mechanisms, and predicting the probability of successful bacterial elimination versus resistance development.
From a systems biology perspective, PhageForge models infection kinetics using mass-action equations and stochastic simulations, capturing adsorption rates, burst sizes, and latency periods. It integrates pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling to predict how engineered phages behave in vivo, including distribution, clearance, and immune interactions. These models are critical for translating phage therapies into clinical contexts, where factors like immune neutralization and tissue penetration significantly impact efficacy.
PhageForge ultimately transforms bacteriophages into precision antimicrobial systems that operate at the intersection of synthetic biology, evolutionary theory, and computational chemistry. By combining structural design, genome engineering, and dynamic modeling, it enables the creation of adaptive, programmable therapies capable of targeting multidrug-resistant pathogens with high specificity while minimizing ecological disruption to beneficial microbiomes.

Cell-Twin
Cell-Twin represents a paradigm shift toward computationally exact digital replicas of living cells, integrating multi-omic data into a unified, mathematically grounded simulation framework. It constructs high-dimensional state vectors for individual cells, where each dimension corresponds to gene expression levels, protein abundances, metabolite concentrations, and epigenetic states. These states evolve over time according to coupled differential equations derived from gene regulatory networks, metabolic flux models, and signaling pathway kinetics, effectively solving a large-scale dynamical system that approximates cellular behavior under varying conditions.
At its core, Cell-Twin leverages probabilistic graphical models and deep generative architectures (e.g., variational autoencoders and diffusion models) to learn latent representations of cellular states from single-cell RNA-seq, ATAC-seq, and proteomics data. These latent spaces allow the system to interpolate and extrapolate cellular responses, predicting how unseen perturbations—such as drug treatments or genetic edits—will shift the cell’s trajectory in state space. Reinforcement learning agents are layered on top of this model, exploring intervention strategies (drug combinations, gene edits) as actions that optimize desired outcomes like apoptosis induction or differentiation.
Mathematically, Cell-Twin integrates deterministic and stochastic modeling approaches, combining ODE-based pathway simulations with stochastic gene expression models (e.g., Gillespie algorithm) to capture both average behavior and cell-to-cell variability. It also incorporates control theory, treating therapeutic interventions as control inputs and optimizing them using techniques like model predictive control (MPC), enabling dynamic adjustment of treatment strategies based on predicted cellular responses over time.
The platform extends into spatial modeling by incorporating partial differential equations (PDEs) and agent-based simulations to represent how cells interact within tissues or tumor microenvironments. This allows Cell-Twin to simulate not just isolated cells but entire ecosystems of interacting cells, capturing gradients of nutrients, signaling molecules, and mechanical forces. Such multi-scale modeling is essential for accurately predicting drug responses in complex biological contexts like cancer.
Ultimately, Cell-Twin enables in silico clinical trials at the cellular level, where thousands of therapeutic strategies can be tested computationally before any wet-lab experimentation. By integrating machine learning, systems biology, and mathematical modeling, it provides a predictive, mechanistic framework for personalized medicine, allowing researchers and clinicians to design interventions tailored to the precise biological state of an individual’s cells.

Fate Predictor
Fate Predictor transforms cellular reprogramming into a quantitatively tractable optimization problem over gene regulatory landscapes, modeling cell identity as an attractor state within a high-dimensional dynamical system. It constructs gene regulatory networks (GRNs) from transcriptomic and epigenomic data, representing transcription factors and their interactions as weighted directed graphs. Using nonlinear dynamical systems theory, it identifies stable attractors corresponding to distinct cell types and computes the minimal perturbations required to transition from one attractor (e.g., fibroblast) to another (e.g., neuron).
The system employs optimal control theory to design reprogramming strategies, where transcription factor overexpression or suppression acts as control inputs. It solves for trajectories that minimize an energy functional representing both biological cost (e.g., metabolic burden, toxicity) and transition time, often using gradient-based optimization or evolutionary algorithms. These trajectories are validated through stochastic simulations that account for noise in gene expression, ensuring robustness of reprogramming protocols under real biological variability.
Fate Predictor integrates chromatin accessibility and epigenetic modification data, modeling how histone marks and DNA methylation influence the accessibility of transcription factor binding sites. It uses statistical mechanics frameworks to estimate the probability of transcription factor binding under different chromatin states, allowing it to predict not just which factors are needed, but when and in what sequence they should be applied to achieve efficient reprogramming.
From a machine learning perspective, the platform uses graph neural networks (GNNs) to learn representations of GRNs and predict the effects of perturbations, while reinforcement learning agents iteratively refine reprogramming protocols based on simulated outcomes. These models are trained on large-scale datasets of known differentiation and reprogramming experiments, enabling the system to generalize to novel cell type conversions.
Fate Predictor ultimately provides a mathematically grounded, data-driven framework for engineering cell identity, bridging developmental biology, systems biology, and control theory. Its ability to map and navigate the complex landscape of cellular states enables precise design of reprogramming strategies, opening new possibilities in regenerative medicine, disease modeling, and synthetic biology.
