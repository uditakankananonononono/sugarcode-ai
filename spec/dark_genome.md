# Dark Genome

Dark Genome: A decoder for non-coding DNA, identifying hidden enhancers and functional RNA elements within the "dark matter" of the genome. It correlates variations in these regions with disease risk and generates novel hypotheses. Dark Genome maps the regulatory landscape, helping researchers understand how non-protein-coding sequences influence biological phenotypes.

Dark Genome can be pushed further into a truly frontier-grade regulatory inference system by explicitly modeling the genome as a probabilistic, multi-scale dynamical system, where sequence, chromatin state, and 3D topology are jointly embedded into a unified latent space. Instead of treating transformer-derived sequence embeddings and epigenomic tracks as loosely coupled layers, the system can implement a multi-modal foundation model trained on aligned sequence–epigenome–transcriptome datasets, where attention heads are conditioned not only on nucleotide context but also on cell-type–specific chromatin states and temporal gene expression profiles. This allows Dark Genome to learn context-dependent regulatory grammars, where the same motif can have divergent effects depending on histone modifications, cofactor availability, or spatial chromatin configuration, effectively moving beyond static motif detection into conditional regulatory logic.
At the architectural level, Dark Genome can incorporate neural fields over the genome, where every genomic coordinate is associated with a continuous embedding that encodes its regulatory potential across conditions. These embeddings can be updated via graph neural operators acting over 3D chromatin contact maps derived from Hi-C, Micro-C, and SPRITE data, enabling the system to simulate how perturbations propagate through topologically associating domains (TADs) and inter-TAD loops. By integrating causal inference frameworks—such as do-calculus approximations within learned structural causal models—the platform can distinguish correlation from causation, predicting not just which enhancer is associated with a gene, but which perturbation will reliably alter its expression under specific cellular constraints.
For variant interpretation, Dark Genome can implement a generative counterfactual engine that simulates alternate genomic states at single-base resolution. Given a non-coding variant, the system generates paired forward passes—wild-type vs mutated—through the full regulatory model, quantifying differential effects on transcription factor occupancy, nucleosome positioning, chromatin accessibility, and downstream transcriptional output. This is augmented by energy-based models of protein–DNA interaction that account for DNA shape features (minor groove width, helix twist) in addition to sequence, allowing the system to capture subtle biophysical effects missed by motif-based approaches. The output is not a single score but a distribution over regulatory outcomes, reflecting uncertainty and cell-state variability.
Dark Genome can further integrate temporal and developmental dimensions by training on time-series single-cell multi-omics datasets (scRNA-seq + scATAC-seq + lineage tracing). This enables the reconstruction of regulatory trajectories, where non-coding elements are assigned dynamic roles across differentiation or disease progression. The system can then predict how perturbing a regulatory element at one time point alters future cell states, effectively enabling forward simulation of lineage outcomes. Coupled with this, a reinforcement-learning layer can explore intervention strategies (e.g., enhancer activation, silencing, or sequence editing) to achieve desired transcriptional states, turning the platform into an active design engine for cell fate control.
Finally, at the systems integration level, Dark Genome becomes a closed-loop experimental–computational platform. It can propose perturbations (CRISPRi/a, base edits, epigenetic rewiring), simulate their multi-scale effects, and iteratively update its models using experimental feedback such as Perturb-seq or CRISPR screens. Outputs are structured as fully reproducible regulatory blueprints, including enhancer maps, causal interaction graphs, variant effect distributions, and prioritized intervention strategies. By unifying deep generative modeling, 3D genome physics, and causal reasoning, Dark Genome operates as a predictive engine for the non-coding genome, capable not only of interpreting regulatory DNA but of actively engineering it to control gene expression with high precision.
