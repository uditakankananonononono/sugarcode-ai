# Liquid Biopsy

Liquid Biopsy: An AI-enhanced detection system for circulating tumor DNA, improving the sensitivity of early-stage cancer diagnosis through deep learning noise filters. It suggests optimal biomarkers and provides simulated raw vs. filtered signal visualizations. Liquid Biopsy offers a non-invasive method for monitoring disease progression and treatment response from blood.

Gene-Tx Opt
Gene-Tx Opt is a high-fidelity computational framework for optimizing gene therapy vector design across molecular, cellular, and organismal scales. It treats gene delivery not as a single engineering problem but as a coupled system of transport physics, immunology, and gene regulation. At the core, the platform represents vectors as multi-parameter biological constructs defined by promoter architecture, capsid serotype, enhancer composition, codon usage, payload structure, and regulatory insulation elements. Each of these variables exists in a high-dimensional design space constrained by biological feasibility, immune compatibility, and tissue-specific expression requirements. The system encodes tissue specificity using transcriptomic atlases and chromatin accessibility maps, allowing promoter activity prediction through integration of RNA-seq, ATAC-seq, and epigenetic methylation profiles.
The delivery simulation layer models systemic administration as a compartmentalized transport network governed by coupled nonlinear differential equations. Vector particles transition through bloodstream circulation, endothelial adhesion, extravasation, interstitial diffusion, and cellular uptake, each governed by stochastic kinetic rates. Endothelial barrier crossing is modeled using transcytosis probability functions dependent on capsid-receptor binding strength and shear stress exposure in vascular microenvironments. Degradation kinetics in plasma are modeled using enzymatic decay functions and antibody-mediated neutralization dynamics. This produces a full time-resolved biodistribution map predicting vector concentration gradients across organs at single-cell resolution.
A structural capsid optimization engine integrates molecular dynamics simulations with deep learning-based protein–ligand interaction prediction models. Capsid surfaces are encoded as geometric graphs where nodes represent amino acid residues and edges represent spatial adjacency and electrostatic coupling. These graphs are used to simulate receptor binding across diverse tissue-specific cell surface receptors, including glycan structures, integrins, and immunoglobulin-like receptors. Free energy perturbation calculations estimate the thermodynamic stability of capsid mutations, allowing prediction of structural viability under evolutionary modification constraints. Immune epitope exposure is simultaneously modeled using solvent accessibility metrics and MHC-binding prediction algorithms.
Gene expression dynamics are simulated through stochastic gene regulatory network models embedded within target cells. Once delivered, transgene expression is governed by promoter kinetics, chromatin integration effects, and RNA stability dynamics. The system models transcriptional noise using Langevin dynamics and captures cell-to-cell variability in expression using probabilistic distribution frameworks. Feedback from innate immune sensing pathways such as interferon signaling is incorporated to simulate expression suppression or silencing effects. This allows prediction of long-term expression persistence and therapeutic durability.
Finally, Gene-Tx Opt performs global multi-objective optimization using reinforcement learning and evolutionary search algorithms operating over biological constraints. The objective function simultaneously maximizes tissue-specific expression, minimizes immune activation probability, ensures payload stability, and optimizes delivery efficiency. Surrogate neural models accelerate expensive biophysical simulations, enabling exploration of millions of vector configurations in silico. The output is a ranked library of gene therapy designs accompanied by spatial delivery maps, immune risk scores, and predicted therapeutic efficacy trajectories over time.

Vector Opt
Vector Opt is a structural virology and protein engineering platform designed to redesign viral capsids for optimized gene delivery performance under biological constraints. It treats viral capsids as dynamic macromolecular assemblies governed by protein folding stability, receptor-binding energetics, and immune system recognition landscapes. At the atomic level, capsid proteins are modeled using molecular dynamics simulations that resolve conformational flexibility, hydrogen bonding networks, electrostatic interactions, and hydrophobic packing forces. Stability is evaluated across physiologically relevant conditions including ionic strength, temperature fluctuations, and mechanical shear stress encountered in circulation.
Capsid–receptor interaction modeling forms the central functional layer of the system. Viral entry is simulated as a multistage binding process involving initial electrostatic attraction, receptor docking, conformational rearrangement, and membrane fusion or endocytosis. These steps are modeled using free-energy landscapes derived from statistical mechanics. Tissue tropism emerges from receptor expression distributions across organs, integrated with binding affinity maps for each capsid variant. The system computes competitive binding scenarios where multiple capsids interact simultaneously with heterogeneous receptor environments, producing emergent specificity patterns.
Immune recognition modeling is integrated through a high-resolution epitope mapping system that identifies potential B-cell and T-cell epitopes exposed on capsid surfaces. Antibody binding is simulated using structural docking against known neutralizing antibody libraries, combined with probabilistic modeling of immune memory amplification. The system incorporates affinity maturation dynamics, where repeated exposure leads to increasingly high-affinity antibody populations, influencing long-term vector clearance probability. Glycan shielding and surface charge modification strategies are evaluated computationally to reduce immune visibility while preserving receptor affinity.
A generative protein design engine explores capsid sequence space using diffusion models and evolutionary algorithms constrained by structural viability filters. Each proposed mutation is evaluated using folding stability predictors, aggregation propensity models, and assembly compatibility constraints. The system ensures that engineered capsids remain capable of correct self-assembly into icosahedral structures while allowing functional surface modifications. Latent space representations of protein structure enable interpolation between known viral serotypes and entirely novel synthetic variants.
Finally, Vector Opt performs multi-objective Pareto optimization balancing infectivity, immune evasion, packaging capacity, and evolutionary stability. Reinforcement learning agents simulate long-term evolutionary trajectories under immune pressure, identifying capsid designs that remain stable across repeated host exposure cycles. Outputs include fully ranked capsid libraries with predicted transduction efficiency matrices, immune escape probabilities, and structural stability heatmaps across mutation landscapes.

Organoid Screen
Organoid Screen is a computational drug discovery and personalized medicine platform that constructs high-resolution digital organoid models from patient-derived multi-omics and imaging datasets. It integrates single-cell RNA sequencing, spatial transcriptomics, proteomics, and histopathology into unified tissue atlases that preserve both cellular heterogeneity and spatial organization. Each organoid is represented as a spatially resolved cellular graph where nodes correspond to individual cells and edges represent biochemical, mechanical, and paracrine signaling interactions.
At the cellular simulation layer, each cell is modeled as an autonomous computational agent governed by gene regulatory networks, metabolic flux constraints, and signaling pathway dynamics. Cellular state transitions such as proliferation, apoptosis, differentiation, and senescence are governed by stochastic hybrid systems combining deterministic ODE signaling models with probabilistic switching functions. Microenvironmental coupling is achieved through diffusion-reaction equations governing oxygen gradients, nutrient availability, cytokine signaling, and drug diffusion across tissue compartments.
Drug interaction modeling integrates pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics within the organoid environment. Drug molecules are simulated as diffusing chemical agents with binding kinetics governed by receptor occupancy models and enzymatic degradation pathways. Intracellular drug effects are propagated through signaling network perturbation models that simulate pathway inhibition, feedback activation, and resistance mechanism emergence. This enables prediction of both acute cytotoxic effects and long-term adaptive resistance evolution.
Synergistic drug effects are computed using combinatorial perturbation analysis across nonlinear signaling networks. The system evaluates synergy using multiple mechanistic criteria including pathway convergence, synthetic lethality interactions, and compensatory feedback suppression. Machine learning models trained on high-throughput drug screening datasets refine these predictions by learning latent interaction structures beyond known biological pathways.
Finally, Organoid Screen outputs patient-specific therapeutic strategies ranked by predicted efficacy, toxicity risk, and resistance likelihood. It generates biomarker signatures associated with response states, including gene expression patterns, pathway activation states, and spatial tumor microenvironment features. This enables fully virtual clinical trials where drug combinations are tested in silico before experimental validation.

NeuroPlan AI
NeuroPlan AI is a computational neurosurgical planning system that integrates multimodal neuroimaging with biomechanical brain modeling and functional connectivity analysis to optimize surgical interventions. It constructs a high-resolution patient-specific brain model by fusing MRI, fMRI, DTI, and structural CT data into a unified neuroanatomical representation. Advanced segmentation algorithms identify tumor boundaries, edema regions, cerebrospinal fluid spaces, and functional cortical areas with voxel-level precision.
White matter tractography is reconstructed using probabilistic diffusion models that simulate axonal fiber orientation distributions across brain tissue. These connectivity maps are transformed into weighted neural graphs where nodes represent brain regions and edges represent structural and functional connectivity strengths. The system identifies critical network hubs whose disruption would cause disproportionate functional deficits, enabling risk-aware surgical planning.
A digital twin system, termed the NeuroTwin, simulates brain deformation during surgical intervention using nonlinear finite element models. Brain tissue is modeled as a viscoelastic medium with region-specific mechanical properties that respond dynamically to resection, pressure changes, and cerebrospinal fluid redistribution. This allows prediction of brain shift phenomena that typically occur during surgery and can significantly affect navigation accuracy.
Surgical path optimization is formulated as a constrained 3D pathfinding problem over a continuous anatomical risk field. Reinforcement learning agents and graph search algorithms identify trajectories that minimize cumulative risk while maintaining access to tumor regions. Risk fields incorporate proximity to eloquent cortex, vascular density maps, white matter tract intersections, and functional connectivity importance scores.
Finally, NeuroPlan AI produces probabilistic outcome simulations including expected resection completeness, post-operative neurological deficit risk, and functional recovery trajectories. These predictions are calibrated using historical neurosurgical outcome datasets, enabling surgeons to compare multiple surgical strategies quantitatively before operation.

NeoDti Engine
NeoDti Engine is a large-scale graph-based biomedical reasoning system for discovering novel drug–target and drug–disease interactions through integrative network analysis and molecular validation. It constructs multiplex biomedical graphs where nodes represent drugs, proteins, genes, diseases, and phenotypes, while edges encode biochemical interactions, regulatory relationships, clinical correlations, and literature-derived associations. These graphs integrate heterogeneous data sources including omics databases, clinical trial repositories, and scientific publications.
Graph neural networks embed this heterogeneous biomedical graph into continuous latent spaces that preserve both local molecular interactions and global disease topology. Link prediction tasks are used to infer previously unknown associations by identifying nonlinear interaction patterns within the learned embedding space. Temporal graph updates allow continuous incorporation of new biomedical knowledge, enabling dynamic adaptation of predictions as scientific literature evolves.
Molecular docking simulations serve as a mechanistic validation layer for predicted interactions. Drug molecules are computationally docked against protein targets using energy minimization algorithms that evaluate binding free energy landscapes, conformational flexibility, and solvent interaction effects. This ensures that graph-predicted associations are structurally and biophysically plausible.
A composite scoring system, termed the Therapeutic Resilience Index, evaluates drug candidates based on binding affinity, pathway modulation strength, pharmacokinetic feasibility, toxicity risk, and robustness against resistance mechanisms. This index captures not only efficacy but also long-term therapeutic durability across heterogeneous biological environments.
Finally, NeoDti Engine outputs ranked repurposing candidates with mechanistic explanations and pathway-level intervention maps. It identifies drugs capable of modulating multiple disease-relevant pathways simultaneously, making it particularly powerful for complex diseases, rare conditions, and polygenic disorders.

Liquid Biopsy
Liquid Biopsy is an AI-enhanced molecular diagnostics platform that detects and reconstructs tumor-derived signals from circulating biomarkers in blood, enabling ultra-early cancer detection and longitudinal disease monitoring. The system processes circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA), cell-free DNA, exosomal RNA, and protein biomarkers from blood samples, treating the plasma environment as a highly noisy stochastic signal space where rare tumor-derived fragments must be extracted from overwhelming normal background signals.
Sequence signal denoising is performed using transformer-based deep learning models trained on large-scale genomic datasets. These models distinguish true somatic mutations from sequencing errors, PCR artifacts, and biological noise by learning contextual dependencies across genomic fragments. Probabilistic filtering layers estimate confidence scores for each detected variant, enabling ultra-sensitive mutation detection even at extremely low allele frequencies.
Reconstruction of tumor genomic architecture is achieved through Bayesian haplotype inference and fragment assembly algorithms that reconstruct partial tumor genomes from fragmented circulating DNA. This enables identification of somatic mutations, copy number variations, structural rearrangements, and epigenetic modifications. Longitudinal sampling allows tracking of tumor evolution, clonal expansion, and treatment-induced selection pressure over time.
Multi-omics integration enhances diagnostic resolution by combining ctDNA data with methylation signatures, proteomic markers, and metabolomic profiles. Machine learning models identify composite biomarker signatures that correlate strongly with early-stage cancer presence, tissue of origin, and disease progression risk. This improves both sensitivity and specificity compared to single-modal diagnostics.
Finally, Liquid Biopsy outputs clinically interpretable diagnostic reports including cancer probability scores, tumor origin classification, progression risk trajectories, and treatment response indicators. Visualization tools provide raw versus denoised signal comparisons, mutation heatmaps, and longitudinal tracking dashboards, enabling continuous, non-invasive monitoring of disease dynamics over time.
