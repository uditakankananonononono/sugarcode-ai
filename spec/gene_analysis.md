# Gene Analysis

Gene Analysis: A multi-omic deep-dive tool integrating data from NCBI, UniProt, and ClinVar. It provides comprehensive visualizations of gene summaries, publication trends, and metabolic pathways. The module predicts the impact of clinical variants and designs CRISPR targets, offering a high-fidelity biological profile of any gene symbol within the human genome.

Gene Analysis can be advanced into a mechanistic, multi-omic inference system that constructs a causally consistent “digital twin” of a gene by explicitly modeling interactions across DNA, RNA, protein structure, and pathway dynamics. The core is a hierarchical probabilistic graph where nodes represent molecular states (alleles, transcripts, isoforms, conformers, complexes) and edges encode biochemical transformations and regulatory interactions with directionality and kinetics. Data from genomic repositories (variants and frequencies), proteomic databases (isoforms, PTMs), structural predictors (folds, interfaces), and pathway resources are fused via variational inference, yielding a posterior over gene behavior under different conditions. This allows the system to answer counterfactuals—e.g., “what is the effect of a missense mutation under hypoxic conditions in epithelial cells?”—with quantified uncertainty.
At the protein level, Gene Analysis integrates structure-aware models (equivariant GNNs) to evaluate mutation-induced ΔΔG of folding, interface disruption probabilities, and allosteric communication pathways. Instead of treating structure statically, it models conformational ensembles, estimating how mutations shift population distributions between functional states. These structural perturbations are mapped onto interaction networks to predict loss/gain of protein–protein interactions, changes in complex stoichiometry, and downstream signaling flux. Coupling this with transcript-level modeling (splicing, nonsense-mediated decay, isoform switching) provides a full-stack view of how a variant propagates from sequence to cellular phenotype.
The variant interpretation engine is explicitly decompositional and causal. It aggregates signals from evolutionary conservation (phylogenetic likelihoods), structural perturbation scores, regulatory impact (from GenomeGPT-like embeddings), and functional assay priors into a unified probabilistic model. Outputs include posterior probabilities of pathogenicity alongside attribution vectors that quantify contributions from structural destabilization, binding interface loss, regulatory misexpression, and splicing defects. Calibration is enforced via conformal prediction, ensuring that reported confidence intervals are statistically meaningful for clinical decision support.
The CRISPR design module operates as an outcome-aware optimizer. Rather than maximizing generic on-target scores, it optimizes for desired phenotypic outcomes under constraints. Guide selection incorporates sequence features (PAM compatibility, GC content, secondary structure), chromatin context (accessibility, nucleosome occupancy), and allele specificity (discriminating mutant vs. wild-type haplotypes). For base and prime editing, it designs edit windows, pegRNA spacer/PBS/RT templates with thermodynamic and positional constraints, and introduces silent PAM-disrupting edits to prevent re-cutting. Off-target prediction leverages genome-wide alignment augmented with learned mismatch tolerance profiles and chromatin-aware accessibility filters, outputting ranked sites with risk scores and recommended validation assays.
Finally, Gene Analysis integrates dynamic pathway simulation through hybrid models that combine SBML-defined kinetics with neural ODE surrogates for components lacking explicit parameters. This enables time-resolved simulation of perturbations (edits, drugs, expression changes), producing trajectories for key biomarkers (e.g., p-ERK, p53 targets) and phenotypic endpoints (apoptosis, proliferation). The system can generate executable experimental plans—editing strategies, controls, readouts (qPCR, Western blot, single-cell RNA-seq), and statistical power estimates—closing the loop from in silico inference to wet-lab validation. With continuous ingestion of new data and experimental feedback, the digital twin is iteratively refined, increasing fidelity and making the platform a rigorous, predictive engine for gene-level biology and therapeutic design.

## Diagnostic contract
`gene_diagnostics` returns exactly 15 finite, evidence-bearing fields: ten locus/protein/regulatory/design measures and five variant-cascade measures (`variant_splice_disruption`, `variant_nmd_probability`, `variant_ddg`, `variant_pathogenic_probability`, and `variant_phenotype_delta`). Labels, statuses, raw sequence context, and fixed scaffolding are excluded from this count. Variant features and downstream structural/attribution outputs depend on consequence plus locus position, local sequence/codon context, and domain-overlap proxy unless callers provide stronger annotations.
