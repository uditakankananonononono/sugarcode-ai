Infinite Diagnosis generates experimental roadmaps and biomarkers, enabling clinicians to find solutions for complex cases using AI.

Infinite Diagnosis
Infinite Diagnosis is a large-scale biomedical intelligence system that transforms fragmented global biomedical knowledge into a unified probabilistic reasoning framework for disease discovery and clinical inference. The system constructs a continuously evolving heterogeneous knowledge graph where nodes represent genes, proteins, metabolites, diseases, phenotypes, drugs, and environmental exposures. Edges are extracted from scientific literature, clinical records, and omics databases using transformer-based biomedical language models trained on full-text corpora, enabling high-resolution semantic relation extraction beyond keyword-level associations.
Graph embeddings are learned using hierarchical graph neural networks that encode both local molecular interactions and global disease–pathway relationships. These embeddings capture latent biological structure by optimizing link prediction, node classification, and graph reconstruction objectives simultaneously. Temporal graph updates allow the system to incorporate newly published findings, ensuring that biomedical reasoning remains dynamic and self-correcting. This continuously evolving representation transforms static literature into an active inference substrate for hypothesis generation.
Diagnostic reasoning is formulated as Bayesian inference over latent disease states conditioned on multimodal patient data, including clinical symptoms, genomic variants, transcriptomic profiles, proteomic markers, imaging data, and environmental exposures. Variational inference methods approximate intractable posterior distributions, enabling the system to compute probabilistic differential diagnoses with calibrated uncertainty bounds. This allows Infinite Diagnosis to move beyond deterministic classification toward probabilistic clinical reasoning under uncertainty and missing data conditions.
A causal inference layer is integrated using structural causal models, enabling the system to distinguish correlation from mechanistic causation. Through do-calculus-based interventions, the system simulates hypothetical perturbations such as gene knockouts, pharmacological inhibition, or pathway activation, and estimates downstream causal effects on disease phenotypes. This allows not only diagnosis but also prediction of therapeutic interventions and disease progression trajectories under controlled hypothetical scenarios.
Additionally, Infinite Diagnosis incorporates retrieval-augmented reasoning, dynamically querying external databases such as clinical trials, omics repositories, and literature archives during inference. This enables real-time grounding of hypotheses in the latest available evidence. Anomaly detection modules further identify rare disease clusters, emergent syndromes, and previously uncharacterized phenotypic associations by detecting statistical deviations across large-scale patient populations, enabling discovery of novel disease subtypes and mechanistic biomarkers.
