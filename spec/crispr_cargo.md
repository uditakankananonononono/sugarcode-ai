# CRISPR Cargo

CRISPR Cargo: An optimization module for delivery systems, suggesting the best LNPs or AAV serotypes for specific CRISPR payloads and target tissues. It generates pharmacokinetic models showing vehicle concentration over time. CRISPR Cargo helps researchers overcome delivery hurdles by ensuring that genetic payloads reach their intended destinations safely and effectively.

CRISPR Cargo expands far beyond a simple delivery recommendation tool into a multi-scale delivery intelligence system that models the full lifecycle of CRISPR payload transport—from systemic administration to intracellular release and nuclear localization. At its core, the platform integrates pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) modeling with nanoparticle biophysics and viral vector tropism data to simulate how different delivery vehicles (lipid nanoparticles, engineered AAV capsids, polymeric carriers) behave across tissue compartments. It incorporates organ-level biodistribution models, vascular permeability coefficients, and receptor-mediated uptake kinetics to predict concentration gradients over time, effectively allowing researchers to visualize how a CRISPR payload disperses, accumulates, and clears in specific tissues under different dosing regimens.
At the molecular level, CRISPR Cargo models vector–cell interactions with high specificity by integrating receptor expression atlases (e.g., single-cell RNA-seq–derived surface protein profiles) to predict uptake efficiency in heterogeneous cell populations. For viral systems, it leverages capsid–receptor binding datasets and structural docking simulations to estimate transduction efficiency and immune recognition risk. For non-viral systems like lipid nanoparticles, it simulates membrane fusion dynamics, endosomal escape probability, and cargo release kinetics using physicochemical parameters such as particle size distribution, zeta potential, ionizable lipid pKa, and PEGylation density. This enables rational selection or design of delivery vehicles tailored not just to a tissue, but to specific cell subtypes within that tissue.
The platform also incorporates immunogenicity and toxicity modeling, which is critical for translational applications. CRISPR Cargo predicts innate immune activation by analyzing CpG content, RNA motifs, and vector-associated molecular patterns that trigger Toll-like receptors or interferon pathways. For viral vectors, it models pre-existing neutralizing antibody prevalence across populations and simulates capsid engineering strategies to evade immune detection. Hepatotoxicity, complement activation, and cytokine release risks are quantified using trained models derived from clinical and preclinical datasets, allowing users to balance delivery efficiency with systemic safety in a quantitatively informed manner.
A key differentiator is its ability to co-optimize payload architecture alongside the delivery vehicle. CRISPR Cargo evaluates how different payload formats—RNP complexes, mRNA, plasmid DNA, or viral genomes—interact with delivery constraints such as packaging limits (e.g., AAV ~4.7 kb), stability, and expression kinetics. It can recommend splitting strategies (dual-AAV systems), promoter selection for tissue specificity, and nuclear localization signal optimization to enhance editing efficiency post-delivery. Additionally, it simulates temporal expression profiles, predicting how long Cas9 or base editors remain active in the cell, which directly impacts both efficacy and off-target risk.
At the systems integration level, CRISPR Cargo outputs fully parameterized delivery blueprints that can plug into experimental workflows or automated biofoundry systems. These include predicted dose–response curves, time-resolved concentration plots, vector composition specifications, and recommended validation assays such as biodistribution qPCR, immunogenicity panels, and in vivo imaging strategies. By unifying biophysical modeling, systems pharmacology, and multi-omic cellular targeting, CRISPR Cargo transforms delivery from a trial-and-error bottleneck into a predictive, designable layer of genome engineering, enabling precise alignment between genetic intervention strategies and their real-world biological deployment.
