# SynBio Wizard

SynBio Wizard: A guided, multi-step pipeline for synthetic biology projects, taking users from goal definition to pathway design and chassis selection. It automates technical decision-making, providing feasibility assessments and assembly recommendations. This wizard ensures that genetic designs are built on solid foundations and are ready for standardized laboratory execution and synthesis. SynBio Wizard: A self-evolving, full-stack biological intelligence system that transforms synthetic biology into a programmable, simulation-driven, and continuously learning engineering discipline. Rather than operating as a linear design pipeline, it functions as a constraint-aware biological compiler that decomposes high-level user intent into multi-dimensional optimization problems across genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolic layers. Given a natural language objective, the system constructs a design manifold constrained by codon adaptation index (CAI), GC content windows, ribosome binding site (RBS) strength (ΔG folding), promoter kinetics, transcription factor binding affinities, and host-specific metabolic burden. It integrates thermodynamic models (e.g., nearest-neighbor RNA folding), enzyme kinetics (Michaelis–Menten, kcat/Km optimization), and flux balance analysis (FBA) to generate pathway architectures that are not only theoretically valid but metabolically feasible under chassis-specific constraints such as ATP cost, redox balance, and precursor availability.

At its core, the platform operates as a “biology-as-code” engine, compiling user-defined goals into executable genetic constructs. It programmatically generates gene circuits using logic-gate abstractions (AND/OR/NOT via repressors and activators), designs plasmid architectures with modular assembly standards (Golden Gate, Gibson), and simulates transcriptional–translational coupling using stochastic and deterministic models (e.g., Gillespie simulations for noise, ODE-based systems for steady-state dynamics). CRISPR modules are integrated at the design layer, enabling automated guide RNA selection using sliding-window + PAM (NGG/NAG) scanning, GC optimization (40–60%), and off-target prediction via mismatch tolerance scoring and seed-region specificity. Variant effect prediction pipelines (VEP-like) annotate mutations with predicted deleteriousness (SIFT, PolyPhen proxies), while structural modules leverage predicted protein conformations to assess folding stability (ΔΔG) and active-site perturbations.
The system extends beyond static design by embedding multi-scale simulation pipelines that propagate perturbations from DNA edits to phenotypic outcomes. A single nucleotide change is mapped to amino acid substitution, structural perturbation (e.g., disruption of hydrogen bonding networks or metal ion coordination), pathway flux redistribution (via constraint-based modeling such as COBRA/FBA), and ultimately cellular phenotypes such as growth rate, metabolite yield, or stress response. SBML-compatible engines simulate gene regulatory networks and metabolic pathways, allowing time-resolved analysis of system dynamics under varying environmental conditions (temperature, pH, nutrient availability). Evolutionary modeling is incorporated through mutation rate simulations, fitness landscapes, and selection pressure modeling, enabling prediction of construct stability, emergence of resistance, and long-term functional decay—one of the most critical unsolved problems in synthetic biology.
SynBio Wizard also acts as a unified biological data infrastructure, integrating heterogeneous datasets from genomic (Ensembl), proteomic (UniProt), and structural (AlphaFold-like) sources into a continuously updating knowledge graph. Each node—gene, protein, pathway, or phenotype—is linked through experimentally validated and inferred relationships, enabling cross-layer reasoning from genotype to phenotype. Experimental outputs (NGS reads, RNA-seq expression profiles, proteomics data) are automatically parsed, normalized, and reintegrated into the system, enabling reinforcement learning from experimental feedback. Protocol generation is automated at a granular level, including reagent concentrations, thermocycler conditions, cloning strategies, and validation workflows (e.g., Sanger vs. NGS), ensuring reproducibility and eliminating ambiguity that currently plagues biological research.
Critically, the system is uncertainty-aware and probabilistic by design. Instead of deterministic outputs, it generates distributions over possible outcomes, incorporating confidence intervals derived from data sparsity, model variance, and experimental noise. Competing design strategies are presented with quantified trade-offs in efficiency, robustness, and risk. The platform can identify regions of high epistemic uncertainty and propose targeted experiments—such as mutational scans, promoter strength libraries, or pathway knockouts—to reduce uncertainty and refine predictions. Molecular docking modules simulate protein–ligand interactions using scoring functions approximating binding affinity (ΔG), while highlighting limitations due to conformational flexibility or solvent effects, preventing overconfidence in purely computational predictions.
Ultimately, SynBio Wizard becomes an active discovery engine that closes the loop between design, simulation, and experimentation. It does not merely assist researchers but augments scientific reasoning—generating hypotheses, stress-testing them against multi-scale models, and iteratively refining them through real-world data. By integrating compiler-like abstraction, systems biology modeling, machine learning, and experimental feedback, it transforms synthetic biology into a discipline that is not just descriptive but truly predictive and programmable—capable of engineering biological systems with the same rigor and reproducibility expected in mature engineering fields.
