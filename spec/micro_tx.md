# Micro-Tx

Micro-Tx: A design platform for microbiome-based therapeutics that pairs beneficial microbes with specific prebiotic fibers to support their growth. It suggests interventions for metabolic disorders and simulates their impact on the gut community. Micro-Tx provides rationales for prebiotic pairings, ensuring microbial treatments can thrive within the ecosystem.

RareNet AI
RareNet AI is a multimodal diagnostic reasoning system designed to resolve rare disease identification by integrating clinical phenotypes, genomic variation data, transcriptomic signatures, and broader multi-omic patient profiles into a unified probabilistic inference framework. The system encodes patient data as a structured clinical–biological feature graph, where symptoms, laboratory findings, imaging results, and genetic variants are mapped into a shared latent representation space. Each node in this graph is connected through learned associations derived from curated rare disease databases, biomedical literature, and population-scale genomic repositories. This enables RareNet AI to treat diagnosis not as classification, but as a probabilistic alignment problem between patient-specific biological signatures and disease manifold embeddings.
At the core of RareNet AI is a hierarchical graph neural network architecture that propagates information across multiple biological scales. Gene-level mutations are linked to pathway disruptions, which are further mapped to organ-level dysfunction and systemic phenotypic manifestations. The model captures nonlinear relationships between genotype and phenotype using attention-based message passing, allowing it to identify distant causal relationships that traditional rule-based diagnostic systems fail to capture. Rare diseases, which often exhibit sparse or incomplete data signatures, are handled through Bayesian uncertainty propagation, ensuring robust inference even under missing or noisy clinical information.
The system incorporates a causal inference engine that distinguishes between correlation and mechanistic causation. Structural causal models simulate how genetic mutations propagate through regulatory networks to produce observable phenotypes. This enables RareNet AI to not only match symptoms to diseases but also explain why a given disease hypothesis is biologically consistent with observed patient data. Counterfactual reasoning is used to simulate how symptom profiles would change under hypothetical genetic or pharmacological perturbations, providing deeper diagnostic validation.
Explainability is a core architectural feature of RareNet AI. Each diagnostic prediction is accompanied by a traceable reasoning pathway that maps symptoms to candidate genes, affected pathways, and known disease mechanisms. This is achieved using attention attribution mechanisms and graph path decomposition algorithms, which extract interpretable subgraphs responsible for each prediction. Clinicians are presented with ranked disease hypotheses alongside transparent evidence chains, improving trust and usability in real clinical environments.
Finally, RareNet AI integrates a therapeutic suggestion module that maps identified disease mechanisms to potential interventions. This includes FDA-approved drug repurposing candidates, gene therapy targets, and pathway-level intervention strategies. Drug–gene–disease interaction networks are used to identify actionable targets, while reinforcement learning prioritizes interventions based on predicted efficacy and safety profiles. The system ultimately serves as a closed-loop diagnostic–therapeutic reasoning engine for rare disease management.

OncoCircuit
OncoCircuit is a synthetic biology design platform for constructing programmable genetic circuits that selectively detect and eliminate cancer cells based on tumor-specific molecular signatures. The system models cancer cells as unique regulatory environments characterized by aberrant promoter activity, transcription factor dysregulation, metabolic rewiring, and epigenetic instability. These signatures are used to computationally identify promoters and regulatory elements that are uniquely active in malignant tissue, enabling cell-type-specific targeting with high precision.
At the genetic circuit level, OncoCircuit designs logic-based regulatory architectures using synthetic biology principles. These circuits are constructed using modular components such as inducible promoters, CRISPR-based transcriptional regulators, recombinase systems, and RNA-based switches. Logical operations such as AND, OR, and NOT gates are implemented at the molecular level to ensure therapeutic activation only occurs under strict combinatorial conditions present exclusively in cancer cells. This reduces off-target toxicity by enforcing multi-signal verification before activation.
The system simulates intracellular gene circuit dynamics using stochastic differential equations and gene expression noise models. Transcriptional bursting, promoter leakage, ribosome competition, and RNA degradation are explicitly modeled to predict real-world variability in circuit behavior. This allows OncoCircuit to evaluate robustness under heterogeneous tumor conditions, where gene expression profiles vary significantly across subclonal populations and microenvironmental gradients.
A tumor microenvironment modeling layer integrates signaling heterogeneity, including hypoxia, acidic pH, immune suppression, and cytokine signaling variability. These environmental factors influence circuit activation thresholds and are incorporated into simulation frameworks that predict whether engineered circuits will successfully discriminate between tumor and healthy tissue. Spatial modeling ensures that diffusion constraints and cell–cell interactions are accounted for in therapeutic activation probability estimates.
Finally, OncoCircuit outputs fully specified genetic constructs with predicted safety and efficacy profiles. These include circuit diagrams, expected activation thresholds, off-target risk assessments, and evolutionary stability predictions under tumor mutation pressure. The system also simulates escape mutations where cancer cells evolve to bypass circuit detection, enabling preemptive circuit redesign for long-term therapeutic robustness.

PDX Insight
PDX Insight is a computational monitoring and validation platform for Patient-Derived Xenograft (PDX) models used in preclinical cancer research. It evaluates how faithfully xenograft models preserve the molecular and phenotypic characteristics of the original patient tumor over time. The system integrates multi-omic datasets, including genomic sequencing, transcriptomics, epigenomics, and histopathological imaging, to construct comparative representations of patient tumors and their corresponding xenografts.
At its core, PDX Insight computes a “Model Fidelity Index” (MFI), a composite metric that quantifies similarity across multiple biological dimensions. This includes genetic concordance (mutation retention), transcriptomic similarity (gene expression correlation), epigenetic stability (methylation pattern preservation), and histological architecture alignment. The system uses weighted similarity scoring and manifold alignment techniques to project patient and xenograft samples into a shared latent space where divergence can be quantitatively measured.
Translational drift detection is a key component of the platform. Over successive passages, xenograft models may accumulate mutations, undergo selection pressures, or diverge from original tumor heterogeneity. PDX Insight models this process as a stochastic evolutionary system, tracking clonal dynamics using phylogenetic reconstruction algorithms. Drift is detected when divergence thresholds in genomic or transcriptomic space exceed biologically defined limits, signaling loss of model validity.
A corrective intervention engine proposes CRISPR-based or selective breeding strategies to restore model fidelity. This includes identifying and reverting deleterious mutations, reintroducing lost subclonal populations, or adjusting growth conditions to preserve tumor heterogeneity. The system models intervention outcomes using simulation-based counterfactual analysis to ensure proposed corrections restore biological relevance without introducing additional artifacts.
Finally, PDX Insight provides longitudinal monitoring dashboards that visualize fidelity trajectories over time. These include drift curves, clonal composition evolution maps, and multi-omic divergence heatmaps. The system ensures that preclinical models remain reliable proxies for human disease, improving translational accuracy in drug development pipelines.

MicroAiverse
MicroAiverse is a computational microbiology discovery engine designed to solve the long-standing “plate count anomaly,” where the vast majority of microbial species remain unculturable under standard laboratory conditions. The system models microbial growth environments as high-dimensional biochemical ecosystems defined by nutrient availability, metabolic cross-feeding interactions, pH gradients, oxygen levels, and interspecies signaling networks. It treats microbial cultivation not as a single-species problem but as a community engineering challenge.
At its core, MicroAiverse uses metabolic flux balance analysis combined with constraint-based ecological modeling to predict minimal nutrient requirements for previously unculturable organisms. Genome-scale metabolic reconstructions are used to infer missing biosynthetic pathways, enabling prediction of essential growth factors that must be externally supplied. This allows the system to design custom media formulations optimized for specific microbial metabolic dependencies.
The system also models microbial co-culture dynamics using generalized Lotka–Volterra equations and agent-based simulations. It identifies symbiotic partners that can supply missing metabolites, detoxify inhibitory compounds, or stabilize environmental conditions. These interactions are simulated dynamically to predict stable microbial communities that support growth of previously inaccessible species.
A reinforcement learning module explores experimental design space, iterating over media composition, environmental parameters, and microbial combinations. The system optimizes for growth probability, colony stability, and metabolic diversity yield. This allows autonomous discovery of cultivation strategies that would be impractical to identify through traditional trial-and-error laboratory methods.
Finally, MicroAiverse enables exploration of microbial dark matter by unlocking previously uncultured species. Outputs include predicted media recipes, co-culture consortia, metabolic interaction maps, and candidate organisms for natural product discovery. This dramatically expands the accessible microbial biosphere for biotechnology and drug discovery applications.

Microbiome Exp
Microbiome Exp is a computational metagenomics analysis platform designed to characterize microbial communities from 16S rRNA sequencing and shotgun metagenomic data. It processes raw sequencing reads through taxonomic classification pipelines, assigning operational taxonomic units (OTUs) or amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) to known microbial taxa using reference genome databases. The system constructs hierarchical taxonomic trees that represent microbial community structure across multiple phylogenetic levels.
Diversity analysis is performed using ecological metrics including alpha diversity (species richness, Shannon index, Simpson index) and beta diversity (Bray-Curtis dissimilarity, UniFrac distances). These metrics are computed across samples to quantify within-sample diversity and between-sample community variation. Ordination techniques such as principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) and t-SNE embeddings are used to visualize microbial clustering patterns across populations or disease states.
Functional potential inference is performed by mapping taxonomic profiles to known metabolic pathways using genome annotation databases such as KEGG and MetaCyc. This enables prediction of community-level metabolic capabilities, including short-chain fatty acid production, bile acid metabolism, and immune-modulating compound synthesis. The system reconstructs functional networks that describe how microbial communities influence host physiology.
A biomedical correlation engine links microbiome compositions to disease states and clinical outcomes using large-scale epidemiological datasets and machine learning models. It identifies microbial signatures associated with conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, obesity, cancer, and neurological disorders. Literature mining components further correlate microbial taxa with publication trends and experimental validation frequency.
Finally, Microbiome Exp generates comprehensive analytical reports including taxonomic distributions, diversity indices, functional pathway summaries, and disease association maps. These outputs enable researchers to interpret microbiome data in both ecological and clinical contexts, bridging the gap between microbial composition and human health outcomes.

Micro-Tx
Micro-Tx is a synthetic ecology engineering platform for designing microbiome-based therapeutics through rational pairing of beneficial microbes with prebiotic compounds. The system models the gut microbiome as a complex ecological network governed by competitive, cooperative, and metabolic cross-feeding interactions. Each microbial species is represented as an agent with metabolic capabilities defined by genome-scale metabolic models and ecological fitness parameters.
Prebiotic design is formulated as a constraint-based optimization problem where carbohydrate structures, fiber compositions, and nutrient availability are tuned to selectively enhance the growth of target microbial strains. The system simulates enzymatic degradation pathways and fermentation dynamics to predict how specific fibers are metabolized by microbial enzymes. This allows precise tuning of substrate availability to favor beneficial microbial populations.
Microbial interaction dynamics are modeled using ecological differential equations that capture competition, mutualism, and commensalism between species. These models incorporate environmental factors such as pH, bile acid concentration, host immune activity, and spatial compartmentalization along the gastrointestinal tract. This enables prediction of how introduced probiotics and prebiotics reshape community structure over time.
Therapeutic effect modeling links microbiome composition changes to host physiological outcomes through systems biology frameworks. Metabolite production profiles, such as short-chain fatty acids and neurotransmitter precursors, are simulated to estimate downstream effects on metabolic disorders, immune regulation, and neurological function. This enables prediction of host-level therapeutic outcomes from microbiome modulation strategies.
Finally, Micro-Tx outputs optimized synbiotic therapies consisting of microbial strains and matched prebiotic compounds with predicted stability, colonization efficiency, and therapeutic efficacy. It also provides mechanistic explanations of microbial selection, metabolic pathways activated, and expected long-term ecosystem shifts, enabling rational microbiome engineering for disease treatment.
