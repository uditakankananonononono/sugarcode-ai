Vector Opt enables the engineering of novel viral variants tailored for specific applications, overcoming common delivery barriers.

Vector Opt
Vector Opt is a structural virology and protein engineering platform designed to redesign viral capsids for optimized gene delivery performance under biological constraints. It treats viral capsids as dynamic macromolecular assemblies governed by protein folding stability, receptor-binding energetics, and immune system recognition landscapes. At the atomic level, capsid proteins are modeled using molecular dynamics simulations that resolve conformational flexibility, hydrogen bonding networks, electrostatic interactions, and hydrophobic packing forces. Stability is evaluated across physiologically relevant conditions including ionic strength, temperature fluctuations, and mechanical shear stress encountered in circulation.
Capsid–receptor interaction modeling forms the central functional layer of the system. Viral entry is simulated as a multistage binding process involving initial electrostatic attraction, receptor docking, conformational rearrangement, and membrane fusion or endocytosis. These steps are modeled using free-energy landscapes derived from statistical mechanics. Tissue tropism emerges from receptor expression distributions across organs, integrated with binding affinity maps for each capsid variant. The system computes competitive binding scenarios where multiple capsids interact simultaneously with heterogeneous receptor environments, producing emergent specificity patterns.
Immune recognition modeling is integrated through a high-resolution epitope mapping system that identifies potential B-cell and T-cell epitopes exposed on capsid surfaces. Antibody binding is simulated using structural docking against known neutralizing antibody libraries, combined with probabilistic modeling of immune memory amplification. The system incorporates affinity maturation dynamics, where repeated exposure leads to increasingly high-affinity antibody populations, influencing long-term vector clearance probability. Glycan shielding and surface charge modification strategies are evaluated computationally to reduce immune visibility while preserving receptor affinity.
A generative protein design engine explores capsid sequence space using diffusion models and evolutionary algorithms constrained by structural viability filters. Each proposed mutation is evaluated using folding stability predictors, aggregation propensity models, and assembly compatibility constraints. The system ensures that engineered capsids remain capable of correct self-assembly into icosahedral structures while allowing functional surface modifications. Latent space representations of protein structure enable interpolation between known viral serotypes and entirely novel synthetic variants.
Finally, Vector Opt performs multi-objective Pareto optimization balancing infectivity, immune evasion, packaging capacity, and evolutionary stability. Reinforcement learning agents simulate long-term evolutionary trajectories under immune pressure, identifying capsid designs that remain stable across repeated host exposure cycles. Outputs include fully ranked capsid libraries with predicted transduction efficiency matrices, immune escape probabilities, and structural stability heatmaps across mutation landscapes.
