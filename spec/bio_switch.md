# Bio-Switch

Bio-Switch: A design platform for molecular biosensors that trigger genetic signals in response to small molecules or environmental cues. It identifies optimal binding domains and couples them to reporter genes. Bio-Switch enables the development of real-time monitoring tools, providing high sensitivity for detecting metabolites, toxins, or biomarkers.

Bio-Switch can be rigorously formulated as a molecular signal transduction compiler that maps continuous chemical input spaces X(t)X(t)X(t) (metabolites, toxins, ions, ligands) into discrete or analog genetic outputs Y(t)Y(t)Y(t) through engineered binding interfaces and regulatory circuits. Bio-Switch optimizes these parameters computationally, enabling the design of biosensors with tunable thresholds, ultrasensitivity, or graded analog responses depending on application context (diagnostics vs metabolic feedback control).
At the molecular biology layer, Bio-Switch identifies and engineers sensing modules across multiple modalities: transcription factor-based sensors (e.g., LacI-like repressors), RNA-based switches (riboswitches, toehold switches), and protein conformational sensors. It uses sequence–structure models to predict ligand-binding pockets and allosteric transitions, ensuring that binding events propagate into measurable regulatory outputs. Integration with structural prediction systems like AlphaFold allows the platform to redesign binding domains, introducing mutations that reshape affinity, specificity, and dynamic range for novel ligands not naturally recognized by biological systems.
From a chemical and biophysical standpoint, Bio-Switch explicitly models ligand diffusion, membrane permeability, and intracellular concentration gradients, solving reaction–diffusion equations to predict spatiotemporal sensing accuracy. It accounts for competing interactions, off-target binding, and degradation kinetics, ensuring that sensor outputs reflect true signal rather than biochemical noise. For small molecules, it incorporates cheminformatics descriptors (LogP, polar surface area, hydrogen bonding capacity) to predict bioavailability and sensor accessibility, bridging molecular chemistry with cellular sensing.
At the systems level, Bio-Switch embeds sensing modules into genetic circuits that amplify, filter, or integrate signals. It enables construction of multi-input logic systems (e.g., AND gates for dual biomarkers) and temporal filters that respond only to sustained signals. Stochastic modeling (via Gillespie simulations) is used to quantify noise and ensure robustness in low-copy-number regimes. The system also integrates feedback loops—both positive (to sharpen responses) and negative (to prevent overactivation)—allowing precise control over signal propagation within living cells.
Finally, Bio-Switch outputs complete, synthesis-ready designs: engineered sensor proteins or RNAs, regulatory circuit architectures, and predicted response curves under varying conditions. It supports applications ranging from real-time metabolic monitoring in industrial fermentation to highly sensitive diagnostic biosensors for disease biomarkers. By merging molecular recognition chemistry, gene regulation, and systems engineering, Bio-Switch becomes a programmable interface between the chemical world and biological computation—effectively turning cells into real-time, adaptive sensing devices.
