# Living Computer

Living Computer: A compiler for cellular logic that designs gene circuits capable of processing biological information. it maps logical operations to genetic parts and simulates signal propagation. Living Computer analyzes stochastic noise, enabling the creation of biological machines that can sense environmental inputs and execute programmed responses with high fidelity.

Cell-Free Opt (Cell-Free Systems Optimization Engine)
Cell-Free Opt operates as a predictive and optimization framework for cell-free protein synthesis (CFPS) systems, treating biochemical reactions as tunable, open-system kinetics rather than constrained intracellular processes. It models transcription–translation dynamics using coupled differential equations that capture ribosome loading, mRNA stability, enzyme turnover, and energy regeneration pathways (e.g., phosphoenolpyruvate, creatine phosphate systems). By parameterizing reaction components—Mg²⁺ concentration, NTP levels, ribosome density, tRNA availability—the system identifies optimal regimes that maximize protein yield while minimizing resource depletion and inhibitory byproducts.
At the biochemical level, Cell-Free Opt explicitly simulates enzyme kinetics and cofactor cycling, incorporating Michaelis–Menten parameters and competitive inhibition effects. It predicts how accumulation of inorganic phosphate, misfolded proteins, or truncated transcripts affects reaction efficiency, and suggests interventions such as chaperone supplementation or energy system redesign. For complex proteins, it models folding pathways and disulfide bond formation, integrating redox control systems to ensure proper tertiary structure formation outside the cellular environment.
From a chemical engineering perspective, the platform optimizes reaction conditions for scalability and cost efficiency. It evaluates reagent consumption rates, energy utilization, and system longevity, enabling design of continuous or semi-continuous CFPS systems. It also integrates lyophilization and storage stability modeling, allowing researchers to design portable, shelf-stable reaction systems for field or diagnostic applications.
At the synthetic biology interface, Cell-Free Opt enables rapid prototyping of genetic constructs without cellular constraints. It simulates expression of synthetic circuits, metabolic enzymes, or therapeutic proteins directly in vitro, providing immediate feedback on performance before transitioning to living systems. This dramatically accelerates the design–build–test cycle by decoupling genetic design from cellular viability constraints.
The output includes optimized reaction recipes, predicted protein yields, kinetic profiles, and cost estimates, making Cell-Free Opt a critical tool for rapid prototyping, diagnostics, and decentralized biomanufacturing.

Stability AI (Multi-Scale Genetic and Phenotypic Stability Predictor)
Stability AI extends beyond sequence-level analysis to model stability across genetic, molecular, and population scales, providing a unified framework for predicting how engineered systems persist over time. It treats stability as a multi-dimensional function of mutation rate, structural robustness, metabolic burden, and environmental stress, integrating these factors into probabilistic models that forecast system behavior across generations.
At the genomic level, Stability AI analyzes sequence features that influence mutation susceptibility, including repeat regions, GC skew, and DNA secondary structures that promote replication errors. It predicts rates of point mutations, insertions, and deletions, identifying regions of constructs that are likely to degrade over time. It also evaluates plasmid stability versus chromosomal integration, modeling segregation loss and copy number variation.
From a protein and biochemical perspective, the system predicts stability of expressed proteins under varying conditions, including temperature, pH, and oxidative stress. It models folding energy landscapes, aggregation propensity, and degradation pathways, ensuring that engineered proteins maintain functionality in real-world environments. Chemical degradation of metabolites and cofactors is also incorporated, linking molecular stability to overall system performance.
At the systems and population level, Stability AI simulates evolutionary dynamics using stochastic models, predicting how selective pressures—such as metabolic burden or environmental stress—drive loss of engineered traits. It identifies conditions under which engineered systems are likely to be outcompeted by mutants and suggests design strategies such as redundancy, kill switches, or selection pressures to maintain functionality.
The platform outputs stability metrics, risk maps, and design recommendations, enabling researchers to build systems that are robust not just initially but over extended operational lifetimes in industrial or clinical settings.

Living Computer (Cellular Logic and Biological Computation Engine)
Living Computer functions as a compiler and execution engine for biological computation, translating logical operations into biochemical processes implemented within living cells. It maps Boolean logic, analog signal processing, and even probabilistic computation onto genetic circuits composed of promoters, transcription factors, CRISPR-based regulators, and signaling pathways. Each logical operation is encoded as a set of biochemical reactions, with system behavior modeled using stochastic kinetics to account for noise and variability in gene expression.
At the molecular level, Living Computer designs regulatory elements that implement logic gates—AND, OR, NOT, NAND—using mechanisms such as cooperative binding, repression, and CRISPR interference. It models binding affinities, transcriptional delays, and degradation rates to ensure that signals propagate reliably through the circuit. The system also incorporates RNA-based regulation (riboswitches, toehold switches) for faster and more tunable responses.
From a systems biology perspective, the platform embeds computational circuits within host cells, modeling resource allocation and interaction with endogenous pathways. It predicts how competition for ribosomes, ATP, and transcriptional machinery affects circuit performance, and optimizes designs to minimize interference. Noise analysis is a key component, with the system quantifying stochastic fluctuations and designing circuits that are robust to variability.
Chemically, Living Computer integrates sensing modules that convert molecular inputs—metabolites, ions, signaling molecules—into genetic signals. It models ligand binding kinetics, diffusion, and degradation, enabling precise control over input sensitivity and dynamic range. This allows cells to function as biosensors or decision-making units that respond to complex environmental cues.
The output includes fully specified genetic circuits, predicted dynamic behaviors, and implementation strategies, enabling the creation of programmable living systems capable of computation, sensing, and actuation. By merging computer science, molecular biology, and chemical signaling, Living Computer represents a new paradigm where cells act as information-processing machines.
