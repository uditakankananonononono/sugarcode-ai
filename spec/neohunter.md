# NeoHunter

NeoHunter: An identification engine for personalized neoantigens derived from tumor mutational data. It ranks peptide candidates by immunogenicity and predicts binding to specific patient HLA alleles. NeoHunter enables the design of personalized cancer vaccines that target the unique mutations of an individual's tumor, providing a high-precision approach.

Tissue Eng
Tissue Eng is a full-stack computational biofabrication system designed to simulate, optimize, and generate 3D bioprinted tissues as multi-scale physical–biological systems. At its foundation, it models bio-ink behavior using continuum fluid mechanics, treating each formulation as a complex non-Newtonian multiphase fluid. Constitutive models such as Herschel–Bulkley and Carreau–Yasuda equations are used to capture shear-thinning behavior, yield stress thresholds, and viscoelastic relaxation under dynamic extrusion conditions. The system simulates nozzle-level physics using discretized Navier–Stokes equations with free-surface tracking to predict filament formation, breakage, and layer stacking stability under variable pressure, temperature, and deposition velocity.
At the materials chemistry layer, Tissue Eng models scaffold formation as a coupled reaction–diffusion system where polymerization, crosslinking, and degradation kinetics interact spatially and temporally. Photopolymerization reactions are modeled using radical chain kinetics, while ionic hydrogels are governed by diffusion-limited binding of multivalent ions. These processes are solved as partial differential equations to predict spatial heterogeneity in stiffness, porosity, and degradation rates. The platform also incorporates time-dependent viscoelasticity models (Kelvin–Voigt and Maxwell frameworks) to simulate long-term mechanical relaxation of engineered tissues under physiological environments.
On the cellular systems level, embedded cells are treated as adaptive agents whose behavior emerges from gene regulatory networks, metabolic constraints, and mechanotransduction signaling. Cellular state transitions such as proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis, and senescence are modeled using hybrid stochastic–deterministic frameworks combining ODE systems with probabilistic switching processes. Environmental coupling is achieved through diffusion-reaction PDEs modeling oxygen, glucose, lactate, cytokines, and morphogen gradients. This enables prediction of emergent biological structures such as vascular niches, necrotic cores, and spatially patterned differentiation zones within engineered tissues.
Tissue Eng also includes a vascular network synthesis engine that formulates perfusion as a constrained graph optimization problem. The system generates vascular architectures by minimizing a cost function combining hydraulic resistance, construction complexity, and nutrient delivery inefficiency. This optimization is solved using graph-theoretic methods such as minimum spanning tree variants, Steiner tree approximations, and flow conservation constraints. The resulting vascular structures are validated through computational fluid dynamics simulations that compute shear stress distributions, pressure gradients, and endothelial mechanobiological response thresholds.
Finally, a reinforcement learning layer integrates across all simulation components, optimizing fabrication parameters such as extrusion pressure, nozzle trajectory, scaffold geometry, and material composition. Physics-informed surrogate models, including Gaussian processes and neural operators, are used to approximate expensive multiphysics simulations, enabling efficient exploration of high-dimensional design spaces. The system ultimately functions as a generative design engine that translates biological specifications into physically manufacturable, structurally stable, and functionally viable tissue constructs.

Infinite Diagnosis
Infinite Diagnosis is a large-scale biomedical intelligence system that transforms fragmented global biomedical knowledge into a unified probabilistic reasoning framework for disease discovery and clinical inference. The system constructs a continuously evolving heterogeneous knowledge graph where nodes represent genes, proteins, metabolites, diseases, phenotypes, drugs, and environmental exposures. Edges are extracted from scientific literature, clinical records, and omics databases using transformer-based biomedical language models trained on full-text corpora, enabling high-resolution semantic relation extraction beyond keyword-level associations.
Graph embeddings are learned using hierarchical graph neural networks that encode both local molecular interactions and global disease–pathway relationships. These embeddings capture latent biological structure by optimizing link prediction, node classification, and graph reconstruction objectives simultaneously. Temporal graph updates allow the system to incorporate newly published findings, ensuring that biomedical reasoning remains dynamic and self-correcting. This continuously evolving representation transforms static literature into an active inference substrate for hypothesis generation.
Diagnostic reasoning is formulated as Bayesian inference over latent disease states conditioned on multimodal patient data, including clinical symptoms, genomic variants, transcriptomic profiles, proteomic markers, imaging data, and environmental exposures. Variational inference methods approximate intractable posterior distributions, enabling the system to compute probabilistic differential diagnoses with calibrated uncertainty bounds. This allows Infinite Diagnosis to move beyond deterministic classification toward probabilistic clinical reasoning under uncertainty and missing data conditions.
A causal inference layer is integrated using structural causal models, enabling the system to distinguish correlation from mechanistic causation. Through do-calculus-based interventions, the system simulates hypothetical perturbations such as gene knockouts, pharmacological inhibition, or pathway activation, and estimates downstream causal effects on disease phenotypes. This allows not only diagnosis but also prediction of therapeutic interventions and disease progression trajectories under controlled hypothetical scenarios.
Additionally, Infinite Diagnosis incorporates retrieval-augmented reasoning, dynamically querying external databases such as clinical trials, omics repositories, and literature archives during inference. This enables real-time grounding of hypotheses in the latest available evidence. Anomaly detection modules further identify rare disease clusters, emergent syndromes, and previously uncharacterized phenotypic associations by detecting statistical deviations across large-scale patient populations, enabling discovery of novel disease subtypes and mechanistic biomarkers.

CAR-T Designer
CAR-T Designer is a computational immunoengineering platform for the rational design, simulation, and optimization of chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapies across molecular, cellular, and tissue scales. At the molecular interaction level, antigen-binding domains such as scFvs or nanobodies are modeled using structural bioinformatics and protein–protein docking simulations. Binding affinity landscapes are computed using thermodynamic free energy models, while molecular dynamics simulations refine conformational stability under physiological conditions. Association and dissociation kinetics are used to estimate binding specificity and predict off-target interactions across homologous antigen families.
The intracellular signaling architecture of CAR constructs is represented as a nonlinear dynamical system composed of coupled phosphorylation cascades and feedback loops. Key immune signaling pathways such as NF-κB, MAPK/ERK, PI3K/AKT, and calcium-dependent signaling are modeled using stiff systems of ordinary differential equations. These models capture activation thresholds, signal amplification dynamics, temporal delays, and exhaustion behavior under repeated antigen exposure. This allows prediction of T-cell functional states including activation, anergy, exhaustion, and memory formation.
A multi-objective optimization framework explores the combinatorial design space of CAR architectures, including extracellular domain selection, hinge flexibility, transmembrane region composition, and intracellular costimulatory signaling motifs. Evolutionary algorithms and reinforcement learning agents optimize competing objectives such as tumor cytotoxicity, persistence, metabolic fitness, and minimization of cytokine release syndrome. This enables systematic exploration of design trade-offs that are not easily accessible through empirical trial-and-error methods.
At the tissue scale, agent-based modeling simulates interactions between CAR-T cells, tumor cells, and stromal components within heterogeneous tumor microenvironments. These simulations incorporate spatial diffusion of cytokines, oxygen gradients, immunosuppressive signaling pathways such as PD-1/PD-L1 and TGF-β, and physical barriers posed by extracellular matrix density. Partial differential equations govern nutrient diffusion and immune cell migration fields, enabling prediction of tumor infiltration efficiency and spatial killing dynamics.
Finally, a translational prediction module maps in silico design performance metrics to expected clinical outcomes using calibrated statistical learning models trained on immunotherapy trial datasets. This allows estimation of response rates, survival curves, and toxicity probabilities prior to experimental validation, enabling rational preclinical design of next-generation personalized CAR-T therapies with improved safety and efficacy profiles.

Living Tx
Living Tx is a synthetic biology platform for engineering programmable microbial therapeutics that function as autonomous living drug delivery systems within host biological environments. At the genetic circuit level, the system designs modular regulatory networks composed of promoters, repressors, riboswitches, CRISPR-based regulators, and synthetic transcription factors. These circuits are modeled using stochastic chemical kinetics governed by the chemical master equation, capturing intrinsic gene expression noise, transcriptional bursting, and resource competition effects such as ribosome allocation and metabolic burden.
Metabolic modeling is performed using genome-scale metabolic reconstructions and flux balance analysis, enabling simulation of cellular growth, energy utilization, and metabolite production under host-specific environmental conditions. These models ensure engineered strains remain metabolically viable while performing therapeutic functions. Constraint-based optimization is used to balance growth rate with therapeutic payload production, ensuring long-term stability within competitive microbiome ecosystems.
Host–microbiome interactions are modeled as multi-species ecological networks governed by generalized Lotka–Volterra dynamics combined with metabolite exchange coupling. This enables simulation of microbial competition, colonization resistance, and ecosystem-level stability under perturbations introduced by engineered strains. The system also models immune system interactions with engineered microbes, including pattern recognition receptor activation and inflammatory response thresholds.
Therapeutic delivery is simulated using diffusion–reaction equations that capture secretion, degradation, absorption, and spatial transport of biologically produced molecules. These models enable prediction of pharmacokinetic-like behavior of microbial therapeutics, including concentration gradients across intestinal or tissue compartments, time-dependent exposure levels, and local efficacy zones.
Safety engineering is implemented through synthetic containment systems including auxotrophy dependencies, kill switches activated by environmental triggers, quorum sensing-based population control, and genetic firewalls preventing horizontal gene transfer. Evolutionary stability analysis uses probabilistic mutation and selection models to estimate long-term genetic drift and escape risk, ensuring containment integrity over extended therapeutic timescales.

NeoHunter
NeoHunter is a computational immunogenomics platform for the identification, ranking, and optimization of tumor-specific neoantigens for personalized cancer immunotherapy. The system begins with tumor sequencing data processing, identifying somatic mutations including single nucleotide variants, insertions, deletions, and gene fusion events. These mutations are translated into candidate peptide sequences using transcript reconstruction algorithms and proteasomal cleavage prediction models that simulate antigen processing pathways.
Peptide–MHC binding prediction is performed using a hybrid approach combining structural docking simulations and deep learning models trained on immunopeptidomics datasets. Binding affinities are computed across patient-specific HLA alleles, enabling personalized antigen presentation mapping. This generates a high-dimensional binding affinity matrix representing potential immune visibility of each mutated peptide.
Immunogenicity scoring integrates multiple biological determinants including proteasomal processing probability, TAP transport efficiency, MHC stability, peptide surface accessibility, and predicted T-cell receptor recognition likelihood. These features are combined into a probabilistic scoring framework that ranks neoantigens based on their likelihood of eliciting strong immune responses.
Tumor evolution under immune pressure is modeled using stochastic branching processes and clonal selection dynamics. This allows simulation of antigen loss variants, immune escape mutations, and heterogeneous tumor subclonal architecture over time. The system predicts how tumors evolve in response to immune targeting, enabling proactive vaccine design that anticipates escape pathways.
Finally, a multi-objective optimization engine selects neoantigen panels that maximize tumor coverage, minimize immune evasion probability, and maximize breadth of T-cell activation. The output is a ranked vaccine design optimized for durability, specificity, and population of reactive T-cell clones, enabling fully personalized cancer vaccine generation from raw tumor genomic data.
