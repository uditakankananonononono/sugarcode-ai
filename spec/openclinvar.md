# OpenClinVar

OpenClinVar: An explainable AI engine that interprets ClinVar variants for researchers and healthcare professionals. It provides plain-language explanations of pathogenicity and generates clinical summaries for decision-making. OpenClinVar bridges the gap between raw genetic data and clinical action, offering patient-friendly reports and comparative analysis to improve variant interpretation.

OpenClinVar evolves into an explainable, clinically grounded variant intelligence engine that goes far beyond static database lookup, transforming variant interpretation into a dynamic, evidence-weighted reasoning process. At its core, the system ingests raw variant data (VCF-level inputs) and maps them across multiple annotation layers—genomic (ClinVar, Ensembl), population frequency (gnomAD), functional prediction (SIFT, PolyPhen, SpliceAI), and disease ontologies (HPO, OMIM). These heterogeneous data streams are embedded into a unified probabilistic framework that applies ACMG/AMP guidelines not as rigid rules, but as weighted Bayesian evidence components, allowing the system to compute posterior probabilities of pathogenicity with transparent reasoning traces.
The engine’s distinguishing capability lies in its deep explainability layer, which uses structured reasoning graphs to decompose each classification into interpretable components. Instead of returning a simple “pathogenic” or “benign” label, OpenClinVar constructs a causal chain linking variant → molecular consequence → pathway disruption → clinical phenotype. This is achieved through integration with transcript-level models, protein structural predictions (e.g., AlphaFold), and curated pathway databases, enabling mechanistic explanations such as “missense mutation disrupts zinc-binding domain stability, impairing transcriptional activation of downstream targets.” These explanations are rendered in both clinician-facing summaries and machine-readable formats for downstream decision systems.
OpenClinVar further incorporates large-scale literature reasoning through semantic parsing of biomedical publications indexed in PubMed. Using transformer-based retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) pipelines, it extracts evidence linking variants to phenotypes, functional assays, and clinical outcomes. Crucially, it performs contradiction analysis—identifying conflicting reports across studies and weighting them based on sample size, experimental rigor, and recency. This allows the system to surface uncertainty explicitly, rather than masking it behind overconfident predictions, which is critical for clinical decision-making in cases involving variants of uncertain significance.
At the systems level, OpenClinVar models genotype–phenotype relationships using graph neural networks that connect genes, variants, diseases, and patient features into a multiplex biomedical graph. Patient-specific inputs (symptoms, lab values, family history) are embedded into this graph, enabling personalized variant interpretation. The system can perform reverse inference—ranking candidate variants most likely to explain a patient’s phenotype—or forward simulation—predicting clinical trajectories given a specific variant. This transforms it from a static annotation tool into an active diagnostic reasoning engine capable of supporting differential diagnosis in rare and complex diseases.
Finally, OpenClinVar is designed as a continuously updating clinical AI system, integrating new submissions to ClinVar, real-world clinical outcomes, and functional assay data into its learning loop. Through federated learning frameworks, it can incorporate data from hospitals and research institutions without compromising patient privacy, ensuring that its models evolve with emerging evidence. In its most advanced form, OpenClinVar becomes a translational bridge between genomics and medicine—providing not just variant classifications, but deeply reasoned, evidence-backed insights that guide diagnosis, prognosis, and therapeutic strategy selection in precision healthcare.
