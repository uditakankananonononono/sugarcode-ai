# Prime Design

Prime Design: A specialized tool for designing pegRNAs used in Prime Editing, enabling precise insertions, deletions, and point mutations. It identifies optimal reverse transcriptase templates and nicking sites to maximize efficiency. Prime Design performs off-target scans and stability analysis, ensuring that complex genomic changes are achieved with high fidelity.

Prime Design evolves into a highly specialized, sequence-aware optimization engine for prime editing that treats pegRNA construction as a constrained, multi-parameter design problem grounded in DNA repair physics and reverse transcription dynamics. At its core, the system integrates transformer-based sequence models with mechanistic simulations of the prime editing process, including Cas9 nickase binding, reverse transcriptase extension, and 3′ flap resolution. Rather than selecting pegRNAs based on simple proximity to the edit site, Prime Design evaluates spacer positioning, nicking orientation, and edit placement within the reverse transcription window to ensure that the desired nucleotide change falls within an optimal editing zone while minimizing undesired byproducts such as indels or partial edits.
A central component of the platform is its joint optimization of the pegRNA architecture, specifically the spacer, primer binding site (PBS), and reverse transcriptase (RT) template. Prime Design models PBS annealing efficiency using thermodynamic parameters such as melting temperature, GC content, and local secondary structure of the target DNA strand. Simultaneously, it evaluates RT template length and sequence composition to balance extension efficiency with fidelity, accounting for factors like mismatch tolerance, sequence context–dependent polymerase pausing, and the stability of the resulting 3′ flap intermediate. This allows the system to predict not just whether an edit can occur, but the distribution of editing outcomes across alleles.
The platform also incorporates detailed modeling of DNA repair pathway biases that influence prime editing efficiency. It simulates the competition between flap endonuclease processing, mismatch repair (MMR), and base excision pathways to estimate the probability that the edited strand is retained versus reverted. For PE3 and PE3b strategies, Prime Design optimizes the placement of the secondary nicking sgRNA to bias strand replacement in favor of the edited sequence, while minimizing the risk of double-strand break–like outcomes. It evaluates nick distance, strand orientation, and timing effects, effectively turning nicking strategy into a tunable parameter within the editing design space.
To ensure specificity, Prime Design includes a genome-wide off-target analysis pipeline that accounts for both spacer-dependent binding and pegRNA-specific risks such as unintended reverse transcription at partially homologous loci. It uses sequence alignment combined with learned mismatch penalty models to identify potential off-target binding sites, and then simulates whether the RT template could feasibly anneal and introduce edits at those sites. This dual-layer filtering is particularly important for prime editing, where off-target edits are less about cleavage and more about unintended sequence rewriting, a fundamentally different risk profile compared to standard CRISPR-Cas9 systems.
At the systems level, Prime Design outputs fully parameterized editing constructs ready for experimental deployment, including pegRNA sequences, nicking sgRNAs, predicted editing efficiencies, and outcome distributions. It can generate design variants ranked by trade-offs between efficiency and precision, and export these as structured files compatible with cloning workflows or automated synthesis platforms. Additionally, it recommends validation strategies such as amplicon sequencing configurations tailored to detect low-frequency edits and byproducts. By integrating sequence modeling, repair pathway simulation, and thermodynamic optimization, Prime Design functions as a high-fidelity compiler for precise genome rewriting, enabling complex edits to be engineered with a level of predictability that approaches rational design rather than empirical trial-and-error.
