# CAR-T Designer

CAR-T Designer: An optimization platform for chimeric antigen receptor constructs, suggesting scFv domains and signaling elements for specific cancer antigens. It predicts potential toxicities and simulates clinical trial outcomes. CAR-T Designer enables the engineering of safe and effective immune cell therapies, accelerating the development of personalized immunotherapies for cancer.

CAR-T Designer
CAR-T Designer is a computational immunoengineering platform for the rational design, simulation, and optimization of chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapies across molecular, cellular, and tissue scales. At the molecular interaction level, antigen-binding domains such as scFvs or nanobodies are modeled using structural bioinformatics and protein–protein docking simulations. Binding affinity landscapes are computed using thermodynamic free energy models, while molecular dynamics simulations refine conformational stability under physiological conditions. Association and dissociation kinetics are used to estimate binding specificity and predict off-target interactions across homologous antigen families.
The intracellular signaling architecture of CAR constructs is represented as a nonlinear dynamical system composed of coupled phosphorylation cascades and feedback loops. Key immune signaling pathways such as NF-κB, MAPK/ERK, PI3K/AKT, and calcium-dependent signaling are modeled using stiff systems of ordinary differential equations. These models capture activation thresholds, signal amplification dynamics, temporal delays, and exhaustion behavior under repeated antigen exposure. This allows prediction of T-cell functional states including activation, anergy, exhaustion, and memory formation.
A multi-objective optimization framework explores the combinatorial design space of CAR architectures, including extracellular domain selection, hinge flexibility, transmembrane region composition, and intracellular costimulatory signaling motifs. Evolutionary algorithms and reinforcement learning agents optimize competing objectives such as tumor cytotoxicity, persistence, metabolic fitness, and minimization of cytokine release syndrome. This enables systematic exploration of design trade-offs that are not easily accessible through empirical trial-and-error methods.
At the tissue scale, agent-based modeling simulates interactions between CAR-T cells, tumor cells, and stromal components within heterogeneous tumor microenvironments. These simulations incorporate spatial diffusion of cytokines, oxygen gradients, immunosuppressive signaling pathways such as PD-1/PD-L1 and TGF-β, and physical barriers posed by extracellular matrix density. Partial differential equations govern nutrient diffusion and immune cell migration fields, enabling prediction of tumor infiltration efficiency and spatial killing dynamics.
Finally, a translational prediction module maps in silico design performance metrics to expected clinical outcomes using calibrated statistical learning models trained on immunotherapy trial datasets. This allows estimation of response rates, survival curves, and toxicity probabilities prior to experimental validation, enabling rational preclinical design of next-generation personalized CAR-T therapies with improved safety and efficacy profiles.
