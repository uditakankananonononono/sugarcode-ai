# Epi-Edit

Epi-Edit: A design tool for epigenome engineering that uses CRISPRa and CRISPRi to modulate gene expression without changing DNA sequences. It visualizes the local chromatin landscape, identifying histone marks and accessibility. Epi-Edit enables researchers to control gene activity through precise epigenetic modifications, offering a non-permanent method of gene regulation.

Epi-Edit extends far beyond a basic CRISPRa/CRISPRi design interface into a comprehensive epigenome engineering platform that models, perturbs, and rewires regulatory landscapes without altering the underlying DNA sequence. At its core, the system integrates chromatin state maps derived from ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq (histone marks such as H3K27ac, H3K4me1, H3K27me3), and DNA methylation datasets to construct high-resolution regulatory profiles around target loci. These profiles are not treated as static annotations; instead, Epi-Edit encodes them into graph-based representations of enhancer–promoter interactions, incorporating 3D chromatin architecture (Hi-C/ChIA-PET data) to identify distal regulatory elements that exert control over gene expression. This allows the platform to design perturbations that operate at the level of regulatory networks rather than isolated promoters.
The system supports a full suite of programmable epigenetic editors by modeling the functional consequences of fusing catalytically dead CRISPR systems (dCas9/dCas12) with effector domains such as KRAB (repression), VP64/p300 (activation), TET1 (demethylation), and DNMT3A (methylation). Epi-Edit simulates how recruitment of these effectors alters local chromatin accessibility, histone modification deposition, and transcription factor binding affinity over time. Rather than assuming binary activation or repression, it predicts graded transcriptional responses based on occupancy models and cooperative binding effects, enabling fine-tuned modulation of gene expression levels. This is particularly critical for genes with dosage-sensitive phenotypes, where overexpression or underexpression can both be deleterious.
A key technical layer involves predicting the propagation of epigenetic changes across regulatory domains. Epi-Edit models how local perturbations spread through chromatin via reader–writer feedback loops (e.g., PRC2-mediated H3K27me3 spreading or acetylation cascades), allowing it to forecast both intended and unintended epigenomic ripple effects. It also incorporates cell-type-specific transcription factor networks derived from single-cell RNA-seq and chromatin accessibility data, enabling context-aware predictions of how epigenetic edits will manifest differently across cellular states. This makes it possible to design interventions that are selectively active in diseased cell populations while minimizing effects in healthy cells.
To ensure precision, the platform includes an advanced off-target regulatory impact engine that goes beyond sequence similarity. It evaluates potential gRNA binding at non-target loci in the context of chromatin accessibility and regulatory importance, prioritizing off-target sites based on their likelihood to influence gene expression or disrupt essential pathways. It also simulates competitive binding between endogenous transcription factors and dCas9-effector complexes, providing a more realistic estimate of effective occupancy and functional impact. This allows users to design epigenetic interventions that are not only sequence-specific but also functionally constrained to minimize unintended transcriptional rewiring.
At the systems level, Epi-Edit generates dynamic, time-resolved models of gene expression following intervention, integrating transcriptional kinetics, mRNA stability, and feedback regulation. Outputs include predicted expression trajectories, regulatory network perturbation maps, and experimentally actionable designs such as multiplexed gRNA sets targeting enhancer clusters or super-enhancers. These designs can be exported into vector architectures for simultaneous delivery and validated through suggested assays like CUT&RUN, ATAC-seq, and RNA-seq. By combining chromatin biology, network modeling, and programmable CRISPR effectors, Epi-Edit functions as a precision control system for gene regulation, enabling reversible, tunable, and context-aware manipulation of cellular identity and function.
