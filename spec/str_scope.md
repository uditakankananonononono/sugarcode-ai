# STR-Scope

STR-Scope: A specialized analytics tool for Short Tandem Repeats (STRs) in cancer and rare disease datasets. It identifies repeat expansions as critical biomarkers and calculates a diagnostic potential index. STR-Scope uses graph neural networks to simulate how STR variations disrupt DNA repair pathways, offering a novel perspective on instability.

STR-Scope can be elevated into a high-resolution repeat-structure intelligence engine that treats Short Tandem Repeats (STRs) not as simple length polymorphisms, but as dynamic genomic instability modules with system-wide regulatory consequences. At its core, the platform integrates long-read sequencing data (PacBio HiFi, Oxford Nanopore) with graph-based genome representations to accurately resolve repeat expansions, interruptions, and somatic mosaicism at single-molecule resolution. By moving beyond linear reference alignment, STR-Scope reconstructs locus-specific repeat architectures—including repeat purity, motif interruptions, and flanking sequence context—which are critical determinants of pathogenicity in repeat expansion disorders.
The system leverages deep sequence-to-phenotype models to predict how repeat length and structure influence molecular function. For coding-region STRs, it models effects on protein aggregation, phase separation, and structural disorder. For non-coding STRs, it integrates chromatin accessibility, transcription factor binding, and RNA polymerase dynamics to predict how repeat expansions alter transcriptional regulation, splicing, or RNA stability. These predictions are powered by hybrid transformer–graph neural network architectures that capture both local repeat patterns and long-range genomic interactions, enabling STR-Scope to infer how expansions propagate effects across regulatory networks.
A defining feature of STR-Scope is its ability to simulate DNA repair and instability dynamics over time. Using stochastic models of mismatch repair (MMR), base excision repair (BER), and replication slippage, the system predicts the probability of repeat expansion or contraction across cell divisions. This allows it to estimate a “repeat instability trajectory,” quantifying how a given STR locus is likely to evolve in somatic tissues or across generations. Such modeling is particularly relevant for diseases where anticipation and tissue-specific mosaicism play a role, providing insights into disease onset, severity, and progression.
STR-Scope also incorporates systems-level modeling of how STR variation disrupts cellular pathways. By embedding STR loci into DNA repair and genome maintenance networks, it uses graph neural networks to simulate how repeat-induced instability affects pathways such as homologous recombination, checkpoint signaling, and chromatin remodeling. This enables the calculation of a “genomic instability index,” linking repeat expansions to broader phenotypes such as cancer susceptibility or neurodegeneration, and identifying potential intervention points within these pathways.
Finally, STR-Scope extends into translational and therapeutic design. It can propose CRISPR-based contraction strategies (e.g., dual-guide excision, base editing to destabilize repeat motifs), antisense oligonucleotides targeting toxic RNA repeats, or small molecules that modulate DNA repair pathways to stabilize repeat length. These interventions are evaluated in silico using integrated genomic and pathway simulations to predict efficacy and off-target risks. In its most advanced form, STR-Scope becomes a comprehensive platform for decoding, predicting, and therapeutically targeting repeat-driven genomic instability—bridging sequencing, systems biology, and precision medicine.
