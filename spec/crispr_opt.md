# CRISPR Opt

CRISPR Opt: A deep-learning-based design tool for CRISPR guide RNAs, optimizing sequences for high on-target efficiency and minimal off-target risks. it includes a simulated genome browser that visualizes PAM sites and regulatory tracks. CRISPR Opt ensures that genomic editing experiments are precisely designed, helping researchers select the most effective sequences.

CRISPR Opt evolves from a simple guide RNA design tool into a fully integrated, deep-learning-driven genome editing intelligence system that operates across sequence, chromatin, and cellular-context layers. At its core, the platform leverages transformer-based architectures trained on large-scale CRISPR screening datasets (e.g., GUIDE-seq, CIRCLE-seq, CHANGE-seq) to model sequence-dependent cleavage efficiency and mismatch tolerance at single-nucleotide resolution. Rather than relying on static scoring heuristics, CRISPR Opt encodes spacer–target interactions using attention mechanisms that capture position-specific penalties, bulge formation, and PAM-proximal sensitivity gradients, enabling highly accurate prediction of on-target activity across diverse genomic contexts and nuclease variants (SpCas9, SaCas9, Cas12a, engineered PAM-flexible variants).
Beyond primary sequence optimization, CRISPR Opt integrates epigenomic and chromatin accessibility layers to model editing efficiency in vivo. By incorporating datasets such as ATAC-seq, DNase-seq, histone modification tracks (H3K27ac, H3K4me3), and DNA methylation profiles, the system dynamically adjusts guide selection based on local chromatin state, nucleosome occupancy, and transcriptional activity. This allows it to predict not just theoretical cleavage efficiency, but context-aware editing probability within specific cell types or tissues. A simulated genome browser overlays candidate gRNAs onto regulatory annotations, splice junctions, enhancer regions, and 3D chromatin interaction maps (Hi-C), enabling researchers to avoid functionally sensitive loci and minimize unintended regulatory perturbations.
The off-target prediction engine extends beyond simple mismatch counting by implementing deep metric learning over genome-wide sequence embeddings. CRISPR Opt constructs a high-dimensional similarity landscape of potential off-target sites, incorporating mismatch position weighting, PAM compatibility, DNA shape features, and thermodynamic stability of RNA-DNA hybrids. It further simulates Cas9 binding kinetics and cleavage probability using biophysical models trained on empirical cleavage datasets, producing probabilistic off-target maps ranked by functional risk (coding vs non-coding, enhancer overlap, essential gene proximity). This enables prioritization not just of “fewest off-targets,” but of lowest biological consequence, which is a much more meaningful optimization objective in therapeutic settings.
To support advanced editing modalities, CRISPR Opt includes specialized design pipelines for base editing and prime editing systems. For base editors, it models editing windows, deaminase activity profiles, and bystander mutation risks, selecting guides that maximize target nucleotide conversion while minimizing collateral edits. For prime editing, it performs joint optimization of pegRNA spacer, primer binding site (PBS), and reverse transcriptase (RT) template using sequence stability models, secondary structure prediction, and repair pathway bias simulations. It evaluates flap equilibration dynamics and mismatch repair probabilities to predict editing efficiency, effectively turning pegRNA design into a constrained optimization problem rather than a heuristic process.
At the systems level, CRISPR Opt integrates simulation outputs into reproducible experimental design pipelines. It can generate fully annotated design reports including gRNA sequences, predicted efficiencies, off-target landscapes, and recommended validation assays. These outputs are exportable as structured files (e.g., JSON, GenBank, BED tracks) and can be directly fed into downstream automation systems or synthesis platforms. By coupling deep learning, biophysical modeling, and multi-omic integration, CRISPR Opt functions not just as a design tool, but as a predictive decision engine for genome editing, enabling researchers to move from raw genomic targets to experimentally validated editing strategies with significantly reduced uncertainty.
